**Title:** An independent 3D-Barolo verification of the CO-dynamical black hole mass in NGC 4061, with a pre-declared resolution caveat and single-axis systematics check

**Description:** Using the provided 12CO(2-1) cube, moment maps, and Band 6 continuum image for NGC 4061, the thesis performs an independent, second-pipeline check of the Nguyen et al. 2026 M_BH measurement, explicitly framed from the outset as a verification exercise rather than a search for new physics.

Before any fitting, the proposal computes the black hole's sphere-of-influence using the paper's own reported M_BH and stellar velocity dispersion: r_SOI = GM_BH/σ² ≈ 50–110 pc for M_BH ~ 1e9 M_sun and σ ~ 200–300 km/s, versus the 0.16″ (≈83 pc) synthesized beam. This gives SOI/beam ≈ 0.6–1.3 — the SOI is at or below one resolution element. This is stated up front as a known, arithmetic fact from the data description (not a "go/no-go" investigation to be run in week 1), and its consequence is built into the thesis's deliverables from day one: at this resolution, a tilted-ring fit may not uniquely break the inclination–velocity degeneracy, so "BBarolo converges to a stable rotation curve consistent with the published M_BH" and "BBarolo cannot uniquely constrain M_BH at this resolution, bounding it only to within a broad range" are both pre-declared as acceptable, complete thesis outcomes — the write-up documents which one occurred and why, rather than treating a non-detection as failure.

The continuum image is given an explicit, load-bearing role: its peak position is adopted as the fixed dynamical center (nucleus/AGN position) in the tilted-ring fit, removing one free parameter and anchoring the kinematic center independently of the CO morphology, following standard practice in gas-dynamical SMBH studies.

Method: 3D-Barolo (BBarolo), a publicly available tool that fits tilted-ring models directly to the cube with built-in spatial+spectral PSF convolution (no custom beam-smearing code required). Semester 1 (with generous buffer, since cube-fitting convergence — masking, initial guesses, primary-beam correction, ring binning — is the primary schedule risk at this resolution): converge on a fiducial fit using the continuum-fixed center, and report whichever of the two pre-declared outcomes results, with residual maps (model minus data) used to check for warps or non-circular motion. The stellar mass profile from Nguyen et al. 2026 is adopted as a fixed input for converting enclosed dynamical mass to M_BH; no photometric decomposition is attempted.

Semester 2, only after the fiducial fit is in hand: the student reads Nguyen et al. 2026's own error budget to identify which systematics it already reports, then runs one single, well-motivated additional axis not covered there (e.g., ring-width/geometry choice, or inclusion/exclusion of channels with residual non-circular motion in the moment-2 map) rather than a multi-axis sweep, to keep the timeline realistic. The final deliverable is a short comparison — fiducial M_BH (or bound) vs. published value, plus the single systematic's effect — explicitly framed as "independent cross-check with a second pipeline, resolution-limited," owning from the start that the thesis's contribution is methodological verification rather than new science. This uses only the supplied data products and one existing, documented tool, and is scoped to fit within a two-semester senior thesis.
