Target: HD 66811 (Zeta Puppis / Naos), an O4I(n)fp supergiant, one of the hottest and
most luminous nearby massive stars, distance ~335 pc. A benchmark object for
line-driven stellar-wind and mass-loss studies, with a long observational history
across X-ray, UV, optical, and radio wavelengths specifically because its wind is so
well characterized at other wavelengths.

ALMA observations: project code 2012.1.00955.S (PI: Samer Kanaan; co-Is: Maria del
Mar Rubio Diez, Jon Sundqvist, Diah Y. A. Setia Gunawan, Nicholas Whyborn, Michel
Cure, Joachim Puls, Francisco Najarro), titled "Mass Loss from Massive Stars II".
Band 6 continuum, ~220-240 GHz. Observed 2014-03-11, publicly released 2015-03-21
(archive-recorded angular resolution ~0.13 arcsec for this execution block). A
companion project, 2012.1.00941.S (PI: Michel Cure), contributed additional band
coverage to the same published study.

Data products: Band 6 (~230 GHz) continuum flux/CLEANed map of HD 66811, one star in
an 8-star sample (HD37043, HD37128, HD37742, HD38771, HD66811, HD149757, HD151804,
HD152236) observed across ALMA bands B3 (100 GHz), B6 (230 GHz), and B7 (345 GHz)
between April 2013 and April 2014.

Science context: this dataset was published in Setia Gunawan et al. 2017, "ALMA
Observation of Mass Loss from Massive Stars," Proceedings of IAU Symposium No. 329
("The Lives and Death-Throes of Massive Stars"), DOI 10.1017/S1743921317002861. The
paper explicitly acknowledges ALMA project codes 2012.1.00941.S (PI Cure) and
2012.1.00955.S (PI Kanaan) as the data used. It presents a pilot study demonstrating
that ALMA (sub)millimeter continuum observations can constrain the wind density
structure in the currently poorly-constrained "intermediate" wind zone of massive OB
stars, where wind clumping most strongly affects mass-loss-rate estimates. Data were
CASA-reduced and CLEANed with S/N > 8; the resulting (sub)millimeter spectral energy
distributions across the 8-star sample were reported as mostly consistent with
thermal (free-free) wind emission, with one (unspecified) target showing possible
non-thermal emission. The stated next step in the paper was combining this with
diagnostics spanning Far-UV to radio wavelengths to derive clumping properties
throughout the full wind.
