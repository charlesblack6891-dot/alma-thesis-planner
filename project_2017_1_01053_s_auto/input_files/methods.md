**Step 0 — Data assembly and bookkeeping**

Start by pulling all six member OUS products for HH 212 from the archive: `Xf93`, `Xfa6`, `Xfb9`, `Xfcc`, `Xfdf`, `Xff2`. Each corresponds to a distinct spectral tuning across the four Band 7 windows (roughly 292–361 GHz in six overlapping steps). Download both the pipeline-calibrated measurement sets and the QA2 image cubes for each — the QA2 cubes are your starting point for line inspection, but keep the calibrated MSs on hand in case any tuning needs re-imaging with a different weighting or cleaning depth. Build a simple lookup table (spreadsheet or text file) mapping each member OUS to its frequency windows, beam size, and channel width, since you'll be jumping between six datasets and it's easy to grab the wrong cube. Log everything in a CASA logbook / electronic notebook from day one — which OUS, which spw, which version of CASA, what parameters — because you'll be revisiting these cubes multiple times as you narrow down apertures.

**Step 1 — Go/no-go field-of-view check**

Before any spectral work, confirm the ACA can actually see both outflow lobes. Look up HH 212's driving source position (given in the data description, RA/Dec ≈ 85.964, −1.048) and its known bipolar lobe extent from existing continuum/CO literature images (e.g., total NE–SW lobe-to-lobe extent). Compare this angular extent to the ACA 7 m antenna primary beam at these frequencies (~30″ FWHM at Band 7, single pointing, no mosaic — confirmed by the "no" mosaic flag in the observation table). Overlay the expected lobe geometry on the primary beam response (a simple Gaussian primary-beam model centered on the pointing center is sufficient — check the pointing center recorded in the MS against the driving source position). If both lobes fall within roughly the half-power point of the primary beam, proceed with the full two-lobe analysis. If one lobe extends past this point, restrict the entire subsequent analysis (apertures, line ratios, velocity structure) to the covered lobe only, and note this explicitly as a scope constraint in the writeup rather than attempting to correct for the missing flux. Do this check first — it determines whether your aperture grid below is one-sided or two-sided.

**Step 2 — Splatalogue verification of the four target lines**

Do not take the data description's line list at face value. For each of the four proposed transitions — SiO J=8–7 (347.331 GHz), ¹²CO J=3–2 (345.796 GHz), SO 8₈–7₇ (344.311 GHz), CH₃OH 7₀–6₀ A⁺ (338.409 GHz) — query Splatalogue (splatalogue.online or the CASA `slsearch`/`spectral-line search` tool) over a window of ±(a few channel widths) around each rest frequency, using the actual channel width for that tuning (~0.94–1.1 km/s, converted to frequency at each line's rest frequency). Record every catalogued transition that falls inside that window, including its upper-state energy and line strength, so you can judge whether it's plausible at outflow/protostellar temperatures (tens to a few hundred K) and abundances. Cross-check against HH 212's known systemic velocity and the outflow's expected velocity range (few to a few tens of km/s local, plus high-velocity wings up to ~100 km/s in SiO/CO) to see whether any catalogued contaminant would be Doppler-shifted into or out of your window. For any line with a plausible blend, either (a) drop it from the analysis if the blend is severe and spectrally unresolvable at ~1 km/s resolution, or (b) flag it and proceed cautiously, checking the moment maps in Step 3 for morphological consistency with the expected shock/jet distribution as a sanity check. Only after this pass do you finalize the four-line (or fewer) tracer set used in every step below.

**Step 3 — Imaging and moment maps**

For each of the four (or reduced) confirmed lines, identify which of the six member-OUS tunings contains it, and extract that spectral window from the corresponding calibrated MS. Use CASA `tclean` in cube mode with Briggs weighting (robust ~0.5 as a starting point, adjustable if sidelobes are an issue given the compact 7 m configuration) to produce a spectral-line cube for each transition, matching or improving on the QA2 pipeline products; if the QA2 cubes are already adequate (correct line, sufficient S/N, clean restoring beam), you may work directly from them and skip re-imaging, using tclean only where re-imaging is needed (e.g., channel range mismatch or excessive residual sidelobes). Continuum-subtract each cube using `uvcontsub` or `imcontsub` on line-free channels identified from the spectral inspection in Step 2. From each continuum-subtracted cube, produce moment 0 (integrated intensity), moment 1 (intensity-weighted velocity), and moment 2 (velocity dispersion) maps using CASA's `immoments`, masking on a signal-to-noise threshold (e.g., >3–5σ per channel, using the line sensitivity values given in the data description as a starting estimate for σ) to avoid including noise in the moment calculation. Visually inspect the moment 0 maps against the known HH 212 jet axis and lobe geometry to confirm each line's emission is morphologically sensible (e.g., SiO and CO tracing the outflow lobes, not scattered noise or a misidentified line).

**Step 4 — Defining the aperture grid**

Using the moment 0 map of the most robustly detected tracer (likely ¹²CO or SiO) as a reference, lay down a series of circular apertures along the known jet axis, centered on the driving source and stepping outward toward each lobe edge, with aperture diameter matched to the synthesized beam (~3.2–4″, use the largest beam among your four tunings so all apertures are directly comparable across lines) or slightly oversized if needed to boost S/N. Given the ~30″ usable field (or half of that, per the Step 1 outcome) and ~3.2–4″ beam, this yields roughly 3–4 independent, non-overlapping apertures per lobe — plan the aperture centers explicitly for this count rather than trying to oversample; treat this as a small, fixed grid, not a continuous profile. Record each aperture's position, distance from the driving source (in arcsec and, using the appropriate distance to HH 212, in AU/pc), and label it (e.g., "N1, N2, N3" outward along the northern lobe, "S1, S2, S3" along the southern lobe if both are covered).

**Step 5 — Spectral extraction and per-aperture line assessment**

For each aperture and each of the four confirmed lines, extract an integrated spectrum from the continuum-subtracted cube (CASA `specflux` or a simple `imstat`/`imval` extraction over the aperture region and full velocity range). Before including an aperture/line combination in any subsequent ratio or kinematic analysis, do a quick visual/S/N check: if the line is not detected above your significance threshold in that aperture, drop that aperture from the ratio and velocity analysis for that line — do not substitute another tracer in its place, and note the dropped aperture explicitly in your results table. For each detected aperture/line spectrum, fit the line profile (single or double Gaussian as appropriate, using CASA's `specfit` or a Python-based Gaussian fit on the extracted spectrum) to measure: peak velocity, FWHM line width, and integrated intensity. Separately quantify high-velocity wing strength by integrating flux beyond a fixed velocity offset from the systemic velocity (e.g., >10 km/s from line center, adjusted based on the typical line width of the narrower tracers) to capture the jet-component contribution distinct from the narrow ambient/wall component.

**Step 6 — Line-ratio and velocity-structure comparison**

Using the per-aperture integrated intensities from Step 5, compute the SiO/CH₃OH and CO/SO ratios (and their propagated uncertainties from the per-channel noise and line-fit errors) at each aperture position along both lobes. Plot these ratios as a function of distance from the driving source to test whether jet tracers (SiO, CO) remain centrally peaked near the source while wall/organic tracers (CH₃OH, SO) show a flatter or spatially offset distribution — this is the core spatio-chemical-differentiation test, executed as a simple ratio-vs-position plot over your 3–4 apertures per lobe, not a fitted spatial model. In parallel, compare the per-aperture velocity structure (peak velocity, line width, and high-velocity wing fraction from Step 5) between SiO/CO and SO/CH₃OH at matched aperture positions, testing whether the jet tracers show systematically broader lines and stronger high-velocity wings than the wall tracers at the same position, consistent with beam-diluted co-location of a fast jet component and a slower shocked-wall component within a single resolution element. Tabulate all ratios and kinematic measurements per aperture in a summary table, and produce position–velocity-style summary plots (ratio vs. position; line width vs. position; peak velocity vs. position) for both lobes side by side.

**Step 7 — Synthesis and reporting**

Write up the aperture table, moment maps, extracted spectra, line-ratio trends, and velocity-structure comparison as a lobe-averaged, coarse-sampled differentiation result for HH 212. Explicitly state, based on the beam size (~3.2–4″) and physical distance to HH 212, what spatial scale of chemical/kinematic differentiation the ACA data can and cannot resolve, and frame the 3–4-aperture-per-lobe sampling as a small number of independent resolution elements rather than a continuous spatial profile. Include the Step 1 field-of-view determination and its consequence for the analysis (one lobe vs. two), and the Step 2 Splatalogue check results (which lines were confirmed clean, which were flagged or dropped and why) as part of the methodological record. Any comparison to higher-angular-resolution literature results on HH 212 should be limited to one or two sentences of qualitative context and must not be used as a quantitative input to the ratio or kinematic analysis — the analysis stands on the ACA dataset alone.
