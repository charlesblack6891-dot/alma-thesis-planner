Target: ultracompact and hypercompact HII regions in the crowded central field of the
massive star-forming region W49A (Galactic), specifically the source catalog imaged in
De Pree et al.'s VLA monitoring field (sources including W49A/G2).

Instrument/data: Karl G. Jansky Very Large Array (VLA), 3.6 cm continuum, B-configuration,
~0.8" resolution. Two (plus a third historical) epochs of nearly identical observations
compared over a ~21-year baseline: August 1994 and February 2015, with the paper's analysis
built around that repeat-imaging comparison.

Source paper: De Pree, C. G.; Galvan-Madrid, R.; Goss, W. M.; Klessen, R. S.; Mac Low, M.-M.;
Peters, T.; Wilner, D.; Bates, J.; Melo, T.; Presler-Marshall, B.; Webb-Forgus, R. (2018),
"Flux Density Variations at 3.6 cm in the Massive Star-Forming Region W49A," ApJ Letters,
July 2018. arXiv:1807.10669.

Publication status: CONFIRMED PUBLISHED (ApJ Letters, July 2018) -- this is a known-published
ground-truth case, not an unpublished dataset to plan around. Data products (calibrated VLA
images, per-source flux measurements) are archival and public.

Science goal (from the abstract): a number of ultracompact HII regions have been observed to
vary significantly in radio flux density on 10-20 year timescales. Theory predicts such
variation when the accretion flow feeding a young massive star becomes unstable and clumpy.
The paper re-observed W49A's crowded UC/HC HII region field at 3.6 cm/0.8" resolution to
compare directly against near-identical 1994 and 2015 epochs. Most sources showed no
significant change; one source, W49A/G2, decreased 20% in peak intensity (71+/-4 to
57+/-3 mJy/beam) and 40% in integrated flux (0.109+/-0.011 to 0.067+/-0.007 Jy).

Data products available for follow-on work: calibrated multi-epoch 3.6 cm VLA continuum
images at matched 0.8" resolution across the full crowded W49A field (not just G2); per-source
peak-intensity and integrated-flux measurements with quoted uncertainties for the full source
catalog; the field overlaps the same target region (and some of the same authors: De Pree,
Wilner, Mac Low, Klessen) as the still-proprietary ALMA project 2024.1.00717.S described in
project_w49a_unpublished/, making this a useful companion/background dataset for that project.

Open items (not resolved, flagged rather than guessed): the paper's introduction text beyond
the abstract (full source-by-source variability statistics, exact epoch count/dates for all
comparison sources, calibration details) was not fully retrieved -- only the abstract was
confirmed via arXiv. A full read of the paper (or a fresh VLA archive query for a more recent
third epoch) would be needed before treating source-count or per-source numbers beyond G2 as
established.
