**Phase 0 — Confirm the published baseline before committing scope (1–2 days)**

Before touching any images, pull the full De Pree et al. (2018) ApJ Letters paper (not just the abstract) and its online tables. Extract three things: (1) the complete list of cataloged UC/HC HII sources in the field with their 1994 and 2015 single-component peak and integrated flux measurements and quoted errors; (2) any explicit statement in the text about which sources are blended, confused, or excluded from clean single-component fitting (De Pree's group has historically flagged the B, C, D, and G clumps in W49A as spatially crowded at sub-arcsecond resolution); (3) the exact calibration and imaging parameters used (restoring beam size and PA, pixel scale, robust weighting, self-calibration steps if any) so your reprocessing uses matched image parameters. Tabulate this in a spreadsheet — it is your ground truth for Phase 3's validation step and the starting candidate list for Phase 1's confusion flagging. If the paper does not explicitly flag confused sources, treat every source with a catalogued neighbor within about 1.5 beam FWHM (~1.2″ at 0.8″ resolution) as a candidate for Phase 1.

**Phase 1 — Acquire and prepare the two epoch images**

Obtain the calibrated 3.6 cm continuum images (or the calibrated visibility measurement sets, if images are not directly distributed) for the August 1994 and February 2015 B-configuration epochs from the NRAO archive / the paper's data products, imaged to the same ~0.8″ restoring beam as used in the paper. If you start from visibilities rather than finished images, reproduce the paper's CLEAN parameters (same weighting scheme, same beam) in CASA `tclean` so both epochs share an identical restoring beam and pixel grid — this is essential, since any residual beam mismatch will masquerade as a flux difference in later steps. Use CASA `imregrid` to place both epochs on a common astrometric grid, and confirm alignment by comparing the pixel positions of several isolated, unambiguous point sources (e.g., ones not in the B/C/D/G clump) between epochs; adjust with `imregrid`/`imhead` corrections if there is a systematic pixel offset. Measure the off-source RMS noise in each epoch with `imstat` on emission-free regions to establish the noise floor you will need for error budgets in later phases.

**Phase 2 — Identify the confused subfield**

Overlay the source catalog positions (from your Phase 0 table) on both epoch images in a viewer (CARTA or DS9). For each cataloged source, measure its angular separation to its nearest cataloged neighbor in units of the beam FWHM. Sources with a neighbor closer than roughly 1–1.5 beam widths, or whose emission contours visibly merge into a single blob at the map's lowest reliable contour level (e.g., 3–5× the RMS), go on your "confused" list; everything else is "isolated." Cross-check this list against the B/C/D/G clump membership from Phase 0. This produces two fixed source lists you will carry through the rest of the project: an isolated-source list (majority of the catalog) and a confused-subfield list (the handful of sources in the crowded core, including G2 and its immediate neighbors).

**Phase 3 — Baseline single-component photometry (validation step)**

For every cataloged source, isolated and confused alike, run CASA `imfit` with a single 2D Gaussian component in a small box region centered on the source, on both epochs independently. Record peak intensity, integrated flux density, fitted size/PA, and the formal fit uncertainties `imfit` returns; add the map RMS and a flat ~5–10% absolute flux-scale calibration uncertainty (matching whatever the paper quotes) in quadrature to get a total error per measurement. As your validation checkpoint, confirm that your single-component measurements for G2 reproduce the paper's quoted 71±4 → 57±3 mJy/beam peak and 0.109±0.011 → 0.067±0.007 Jy integrated values to within their quoted uncertainties. Do not proceed to Phase 4 until this validation passes — it confirms your imaging, regridding, and fitting pipeline is faithful to the published methodology. For the isolated-source list, this single-component table is your final photometry; no further reprocessing is needed for those sources.

**Phase 4 — Multi-component deblending of the confused subfield**

For each group on the confused list (e.g., the B/C/D/G clump members and G2's immediate neighbors), construct a joint multi-component `imfit` call in CASA that fits N simultaneous 2D Gaussians in a single bounding box covering the whole blended group, one component per known source in the group. Seed each component's initial position and size from the Phase 0 catalog coordinates so the fit converges to physically sensible solutions rather than arbitrary local minima. Run this simultaneous fit independently on each epoch. After each fit, subtract the model from the image (`imfit` can output a residual image) and inspect it: a good deblend leaves a residual consistent with the local noise, with no significant leftover flux or holes; if the residual shows structure, adjust initial guesses, free up size/PA parameters, or add a low-order background component and refit. Record deblended peak intensity, integrated flux, and their formal fit-covariance uncertainties (again combined in quadrature with map RMS and the calibration error) for every member of every confused group, in both epochs.

**Phase 5 — Quantify the single- vs. multi-component bias**

Build a per-source, per-epoch comparison table restricted to the confused subfield: single-component flux (from Phase 3) versus deblended flux (from Phase 4), for both peak intensity and integrated flux. Compute the fractional difference (deblended − single)/deblended for each measurement, and express it also in units of the single-component measurement's quoted uncertainty, so you can state whether the bias is significant relative to the errors the paper would have quoted. Look for a systematic direction and magnitude across the group — e.g., whether blending consistently inflates integrated flux (excess flux borrowed from a neighbor's wings) while suppressing peak intensity (beam dilution), and whether the size of this effect scales with how close a pair's separation is to the beam FWHM. Summarize this as a table and a simple scatter plot (single-component bias fraction vs. angular separation/beam FWHM) — this is the core deliverable answering the bounded methods question.

**Phase 6 — Reassess epoch-to-epoch variability with corrected fluxes**

For every source in the confused subfield, recompute the 1994→2015 flux difference and its significance twice: once using the Phase 3 single-component values (reproducing what a naive census would report) and once using the Phase 4 deblended values. Use the same significance metric implied by the paper's own G2 result, i.e., Δ = (F_2015 − F_1994)/√(σ_2015² + σ_1994²), and adopt a consistent threshold (e.g., |Δ| ≥ 3) to classify each source as "significantly variable" or "consistent with no change" under each method. Tabulate the two classifications side by side for every confused-subfield source, including G2 itself, and flag any source whose classification flips between the single-component and deblended analysis. Specifically check whether G2's neighbor(s) in the deblended fit absorb or contribute flux in a way that changes G2's own significance relative to the published number.

**Phase 7 — Write-up**

Assemble the final deliverable as: (1) the confusion-flagging criterion and resulting subfield source list (Phase 2); (2) the validated single-component table matching the paper's G2 numbers (Phase 3); (3) the deblended multi-component table with residual-map diagnostics (Phase 4); (4) the bias-quantification table and figure (Phase 5); and (5) the dual variability classification table showing which, if any, sources change status once deblending is applied (Phase 6). Present this as a self-contained methods correction to the crowded-field photometry used in the source paper, using only the two archival 1994 and 2015 3.6 cm VLA epochs already in hand.
