**A tiered, archive-audited kinematic-degeneracy study of hypercompact HII regions in W49N, using only already-public prior VLA 7 mm and ALMA 1.2 mm/H29α data**

This thesis remains fully independent of the proprietary, unexecuted 2024.1.00717.S Band 9 data, and is structured so that its viability does not rest on unverified assumptions about the older datasets — those assumptions are converted into an explicit, time-boxed audit with pre-defined fallback tracks, rather than being presumed.

**Phase 0 — Data audit (weeks 1–4, hard deadline, itself a graded milestone):** the student directly queries the NRAO VLA archive and the ALMA archive for the W49A/W49N field to (a) identify the specific project code(s) that produced the VLA 7 mm continuum and ALMA 1.2 mm continuum/H29α data cited in the 2024.1.00717.S proposal text, (b) confirm public release status, and (c) determine whether pipeline-calibrated, science-ready image/cube products exist for direct download versus only calibrated visibilities (measurement sets). The audit's outcome routes the thesis down one of three pre-specified tracks, so no outcome leaves the student without a defined path:

- **Track A (images available):** if calibrated image-level H29α cubes + continuum exist for ≥1 catalogued HC HII source in both datasets, proceed directly to kinematic analysis.
- **Track B (only calibrated visibilities available):** ALMA archive delivery routinely includes pipeline-calibrated measurement sets even when quick-look images are not separately archived; if that is the case here, the student performs standard `tclean` imaging from the existing calibration tables (routine imaging, not manual calibration/self-cal) for the 1–2 sources needed — a bounded, ~1–2 week task, not a redesign.
- **Track C (no usable image-quality data for either instrument):** if neither dataset yields usable calibrated products, the thesis pivots to a fully archival, image-only reanalysis of De Pree et al.'s already-published VLA/ALMA figures and reported flux/velocity measurements, sufficient for the same downstream model-comparison framework at lower resolution.

**Phase 1 (kinematic analysis, given data in hand from whichever track applies):** extract the H29α position–velocity diagram and continuum emission-measure profile for the selected source(s), and fit three analytic kinematic templates (rotating disk, expanding shell, biconical outflow) across a grid of inclinations using chi-square/AIC model comparison, as in the prior iteration.

**Built-in resolution fallback:** if the resulting PV diagrams span only a few resolution elements — plausible, since the proposal itself describes this regime as resolution-limited — the deliverable shifts from "which model wins" to a Monte Carlo requirements analysis: synthetic disk/shell/outflow signals are injected at the data's actual beam size and noise level, and the student quantifies the resolution/SNR threshold needed to discriminate the models at a stated confidence. This is itself a complete, well-defined, single-student-scope deliverable, and it directly and quantitatively motivates the PI team's Band 9 follow-up rationale.

The thesis is judged a success either by reaching a statistically grounded disk/shell/outflow verdict for the chosen source(s), or by rigorously quantifying why and by how much the existing data cannot yet resolve the question — both are pre-specified, bounded outcomes reachable with standard CASA/analytic/Monte Carlo tools and zero dependency on the unexecuted, proprietary 2024.1.00717.S observations.
