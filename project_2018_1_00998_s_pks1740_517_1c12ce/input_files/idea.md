**Title:** Spatially Resolving CO(2–1) Absorption Against the Double Radio Core of PKS1740–517: One Component or Two?

**Idea:** Using the publicly available, pipeline-calibrated Band 4 long-baseline (0.034″) ALMA data and QA2 image/cube products from project 2018.1.00998.S, characterize the mm continuum morphology of PKS1740-517 and determine whether the CO(2–1) absorption line (rest 230.538 GHz; falls at an observed frequency of ≈160.10 GHz in the 159.0–160.9 GHz spectral window, assuming the quoted host redshift z = 0.44 — treat this as provisional and verify against Splatalogue before any line identification or imaging) is seen against one or both components of the continuum source. The student would: (1) download the calibrated measurement set and QA2 continuum image for the single member OUS, (2) use the two continuum-only windows (145.0–149.0 GHz observed) to build a deep, high-fidelity continuum map at 0.034″ resolution and identify whether the background source resolves into one or two compact components (testing the "double core" merger scenario suggested in the proposal abstract), (3) extract absorption spectra at each identified continuum peak location from the 159.0–160.9 GHz cube using CASA, and (4) compare line depth, centroid velocity, and width between sightlines to determine whether the absorbing gas has a single, spatially uniform kinematic signature (consistent with a compact circumnuclear disk/torus) or differs between components (consistent with extended, merger-disturbed gas). This is a focused measurement-and-comparison project built entirely on existing pipeline products plus modest CASA re-imaging/spectral extraction — not new calibration or observation — and is scoped to answer a single, well-defined geometric question (one absorbing screen or two) rather than building a full kinematic model of the system.
