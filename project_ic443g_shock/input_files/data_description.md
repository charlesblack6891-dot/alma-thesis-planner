Target: IC 443 clump G (IC443G), a shocked molecular clump within the supernova
remnant IC 443 (the "Jellyfish Nebula"), a Galactic core-collapse SNR at a distance of
roughly 1.5-2 kpc, age uncertain (~3-30 x 10^3 yr). Clump G sits near the center of the
optical remnant and forms the western-most edge of a ring of shocked molecular gas
traced in CO, HCO+, and H2 emission.

ALMA observations: project code 2025.1.00086.S (PI: Tu), Band 5, two associated
pointings (IC443G and an off-source reference position IC443G_OFF_0). Per the ALMA
archive, this project's data-release date is 2027-07-10 -- more than a year in the
future as of 2026-07-22, so this dataset remains proprietary to the PI team; this is a
structural (proprietary-lock) conclusion, not an inference from an absent literature
search.

Prior characterization of this target (from earlier, non-ALMA observations -- no
interferometric millimeter imaging of IC 443G was found in the sources used to build
this description): decades of single-dish and other-wavelength study established IC
443 as one of the best-studied cases of supernova remnant / molecular cloud
interaction. Classic multitransition single-dish studies (e.g. Turner et al. 1992)
mapped shock chemistry toward clump G in CO, HCO+, HCN, CN, SiO, CS, SO, and H2CO,
and broad-line (~10 km/s FWHM) shocked atomic carbon [CI] emission has been reported
with a spatial distribution similar to 12CO. The remnant's shocked gas cannot be
explained by a single shock type and requires a mixture of J-type (fast, dissociative)
and C-type (slower, non-dissociative) shocks.

Note for Stage 6 (novelty-check) testing: unlike the other "unpublished" cases in this

appears to be genuinely new territory, not just a new project code on an
