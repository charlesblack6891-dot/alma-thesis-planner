# Quantitative Shock-Model Fitting to Archival Multi-Transition Line Data Toward IC443G

## Abstract

The interstellar shock (or shock mixture) responsible for the molecular line emission observed toward clump G in the supernova remnant IC443 has been qualitatively characterized in the literature as requiring a combination of J-type and C-type shocks, but this claim has not been quantitatively tested against a self-consistent excitation and shock-chemistry analysis. This thesis addresses that gap using only already-public data: the published multi-transition single-dish line measurements toward clump G (CO, HCO+, HCN, CN, SiO, CS, SO, H2CO from Turner, Xu & Rieu 1992 and related literature) and the published broad-line [CI] detection. No new observations or proprietary data are required. The work derives densities and column densities for each species via non-LTE excitation modeling, fits both single-shock-type and mixed (J+C) shock-model grids to the resulting abundances and abundance ratios, and performs a formal statistical model comparison to determine whether a shock mixture is actually required by the data or whether a single shock type with a range of parameters suffices. The deliverable is a compiled archival line-ratio dataset, a set of excitation solutions, and a best-fit shock-parameter model with quantitative goodness-of-fit comparison — a bounded, one-semester-to-one-year thesis that refines and quantitatively tests the existing qualitative picture of clump G's shocked gas.

## Work Plan

**1. Assemble the archival line dataset.** Build a homogeneous table (astropy Table, CSV/ECSV) of every published single-dish measurement toward clump G — primarily Turner, Xu & Rieu (1992) — recording, per transition of CO, HCO+, HCN, CN, SiO, CS, SO, H2CO, and the [CI] line: intensity (noting Tmb vs. Ta*), LSR velocity, FWHM linewidth, and beam size. Keep narrow (~few km/s) and broad (~10 km/s) velocity components as separate rows. Apply beam-dilution corrections for a consistent solid angle, and retain a provenance column (paper, table) for traceability. *Key output: a clean, provenance-tracked master line-intensity table.*

**2. Derive densities and column densities (RADEX).** For each species, run RADEX (e.g., via SpectralRadex) with LAMDA collisional rates to solve for n(H2) and column density reproducing observed line ratios, separately for narrow and broad components, over a grid in density/column density at fixed temperature steps. Identify χ²-minimizing solutions and uncertainties. Tabulate n(H2), N(species), and fractional abundances relative to N(H2) (CO as total-gas proxy). *Key output: table of best-fit densities/column densities/abundances per species and velocity component, with uncertainty regions.*

**3. Fit single-shock-type models.** Using a public parametrized shock-model grid (Paris-Durham C-shock/J-shock code, or Flower & Pineau des Forêts-type tabulations) spanning shock velocity, pre-shock density, and magnetic-field scaling, grid-search fit (χ² minimization) the observed abundance ratios (SiO/CO, HCO+/CO, CN/HCN, SO/CO, H2CO/CO) separately against the C-shock and J-shock grids. Record per-species residuals. *Key output: best-fit single-C-shock and single-J-shock parameters, plus a per-species residual plot revealing which tracers (e.g., SiO) a single shock type fails to reproduce.*

**4. Fit a mixed (C+J) shock model.** Model each species' abundance as a weighted combination of C-shock and J-shock grid predictions, with shock parameters and relative beam-filling weight as free parameters. Anchor the fit using the narrow/broad velocity decomposition (broad → J-shock/high-velocity C-shock; narrow → lower-velocity C-shock/ambient gas) rather than a free-floating statistical fit. *Key output: best-fit mixture parameters and weights.*

**5. Statistical model comparison.** Compare single-C, single-J, and mixture fits using reduced χ², AIC/BIC, and/or Bayesian evidence (if MCMC, e.g. emcee, is used for parameter search), penalizing the mixture model's extra degrees of freedom. Present the comparison species-by-species and in aggregate as the thesis's central quantitative result. *Key output: model-comparison table/plot (χ²/AIC/BIC by model) determining whether the mixture is statistically required.*

**6. Synthesize best-fit parameters.** Report best-fit shock velocity, pre-shock density, and magnetic-field scaling (with propagated uncertainties) for the statistically preferred model(s), alongside the RADEX densities/column densities, as a self-consistent physical picture of clump G. Explicitly state whether the reanalysis confirms, refines, or fails to require the literature's J+C mixture claim. All analysis (RADEX runs, grid-fitting, plotting) should be scripted/notebooked in Python (astropy, SpectralRadex, least-squares/MCMC libraries) for full reproducibility. *Key output: final summary table/figure of best-fit shock parameters per surviving model, framed as a quantitative test of the existing qualitative claim.*

## Background Reading
