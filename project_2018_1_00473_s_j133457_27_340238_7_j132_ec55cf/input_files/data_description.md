## Proposal abstract

JINGLE is a new large program on the JCMT that is mapping 193 nearby spiral galaxies. The sample has been selected to have extensive ancillary data from the UV to the far-infrared as well as resolved integral-field optical spectroscopy. We propose to use the stand-alone ACA to make CO(2-1) maps of the 20 brightest JINGLE targets observed so far in CO(2-1) with the JCMT. These ACA maps will allow us to measure the angular extent and radial profile of the CO emission, which will produce a more accurate estimate of the total molecular gas mass as well as resolve its spatial distribution, kinematics, and gas-to-dust ratio. We will also investigate the resolved Kennicutt-Schmidt law and CO Tully-Fisher relation. These JINGLE galaxies are interesting targets for the ACA because they are more gas rich than other resolved nearby galaxy surveys and already have global CO, HI, and dust data available.

## Scientific context (LLM annotation)

### Likely spectral lines in the coverage

- **CO J=2–1** (¹²C¹⁶O, rest 230.538 GHz) — the deliberately targeted line for every member OUS. The tabulated ~2 GHz windows (220.4–227.7 GHz across the sample) bracket CO(2–1) redshifted by roughly z ≈ 0.013–0.047, consistent with JINGLE's SDSS-selected nearby-spiral sample (typical z ≲ 0.05, median ~0.02–0.03). Each individual OUS window is centered on that target's own systemic velocity — e.g., the 220.7–222.7 GHz tuning for J132443+323225 implies z≈0.040, while the 225.7–227.7 GHz tuning for J132051+312159 implies z≈0.017. I assumed the per-target redshift implied directly by the band center of each row rather than a single survey-wide value, since JINGLE spans a range of nearby-universe redshifts.
- No other bright mm-wave line falls in these passbands at the implied redshifts: ¹³CO(2–1) (220.399 GHz), C¹⁸O(2–1) (219.560 GHz), and the CN N=2–1 hyperfine complex (226.874/226.660 GHz) are all offset from CO(2–1) by 4–11 GHz in rest frequency, well outside each ~2 GHz observed window. Serendipitous line detection is therefore unlikely; the remaining bandwidth in each spectral window is effectively continuum-only.

### Scientific context

CO(2–1) is the standard tracer of the bulk molecular gas reservoir in normal star-forming disks, and with a CO-to-H₂ conversion factor it converts directly into total H₂ mass, radial gas surface-density profiles, and rotational kinematics — exactly the measurements this proposal seeks for its 20 JINGLE targets. Using the ACA alone (rather than the full 12-m array) is a deliberate choice to recover extended, low-surface-brightness disk emission that longer baselines would resolve out, which matters for cross-calibrating against the single-dish JCMT CO(2–1) fluxes that originally selected these sources and for obtaining accurate total gas masses. The ~5–6″ angular resolution (a few kpc at these redshifts) is coarse compared to a 12-m array but adequate to resolve the radial CO profile and rotation curve needed for the resolved Kennicutt–Schmidt law and CO Tully–Fisher tests described in the abstract. The Band 6 continuum sensitivity (~0.3–0.7 mJy/beam) simultaneously captures rest-frame ~1.2 mm thermal dust continuum, giving a resolved gas-to-dust ratio when combined with JINGLE's existing far-IR/UV-derived dust and SFR maps. Because each spectral window is narrow and centered solely on redshifted CO(2–1), the science payoff here is purely spatial and kinematic (extent, profile, velocity field, and continuum) rather than multi-line chemical or excitation diagnostics.
