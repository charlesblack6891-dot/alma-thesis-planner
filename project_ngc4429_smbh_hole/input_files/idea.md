**Title: Radial Profiling of Dense Molecular Gas and Continuum Emission in the Nucleus of NGC 4429: A Beam-Limited, Sensitivity-Aware Test for a Circumnuclear HCN(1–0) Deficit**

Using only the two delivered, science-ready ALMA products (the 320×320×126 HCN(1–0) cube and the Band 3 continuum image), the student maps the distribution and kinematics of dense molecular gas in the central few hundred parsecs of NGC 4429 and tests for a central depression in this higher-critical-density tracer, self-defining "circumnuclear" from the pipeline's own given nucleus coordinates and beam sizes rather than any externally reported hole geometry.

**Explicit scoping (stated up front in the thesis, not discovered mid-project):**
- At the Virgo Cluster distance (~16.5 Mpc, ~80 pc/arcsec), the HCN(1–0) beam (~0.52″×0.50″) corresponds to ~40–45 pc — comparable to or larger than the ~10–100 pc cavity described in the proposal's background text. The thesis therefore answers "is there a beam-scale central deficit in dense gas?", not "is the true sub-beam cavity devoid of gas?" A null result (no resolved dip, or a beam-diluted/marginal detection) is a legitimate, reportable outcome, not a failed thesis.
- The HCN cube is explicitly a "representative bandwidth" pipeline product, meaning reduced velocity coverage relative to a full science-bandwidth cube. The thesis flags this up front as a sensitivity/coverage caveat: any non-detection could reflect narrow bandwidth suppressing broad-line flux (e.g. from a disturbed or high-dispersion nuclear component) rather than a genuine absence of dense gas, and this ambiguity is reported explicitly wherever a limit is used to argue for a deficit.
- Given that HCN(1–0) is a dense-gas tracer and SB0 galaxies are not typically HCN-bright, the thesis is planned around the realistic expectation that most or all of the radial profile — not just the nuclear aperture — may yield only 3σ upper limits. The analysis plan and writeup structure treat "upper limits everywhere" as the primary anticipated outcome, with a detection at any radius treated as a bonus result, not the baseline the project depends on for success.

Concretely:
1. Build moment 0/1/2 maps and a position–velocity diagram from the HCN(1–0) cube using standard spectral-cube/CASA tools, after basic noise characterization and masking.
2. Center concentric circular apertures on the given nucleus position (RA = 12:27:26.503, Dec = +11:06:27.58), scaled to the cube's synthesized beam (radii of 1, 2, 3 beam-widths), and extract an azimuthally averaged radial intensity profile of HCN(1–0) outward through the circumnuclear disk.
3. Test for a central depression quantitatively: report either a nuclear-aperture flux measurement or a 3σ upper limit at each radius. Convert detections/limits to an HCN luminosity and dense-gas mass (or mass limit) using a standard, generically cited α_HCN conversion factor, used purely as a unit-conversion tool rather than a target-specific literature number.
4. Use the velocity field/PV diagram to check whether any detected dense gas is dynamically consistent with ordered rotation in the known circumnuclear disk versus an inflow/outflow signature, as a secondary diagnostic.
5. Cross-check with the Band 3 continuum image: measure nuclear continuum flux density in the same beam-scaled aperture to test for a compact core (possible AGN/dust signature) versus a continuum-faint nucleus.

This is a self-contained imaging/photometry/kinematics analysis of the two named data products, uses no external catalogs or unnamed literature values, and yields a clear, falsifiable, resolution- and sensitivity-aware result — well within a one-semester-to-one-year thesis scope.
