**Title: A shock-tracer census of the Antennae overlap region: what fraction of column-selected dense clumps show SiO/HNCO, and where does the shocked gas sit in velocity?**

**Data.** ALMA 2013.1.01041.S alone (public since 2015-11-12; one member OUS `uid://A001/X12a/X4e`; Band 6 mosaic of the Antennae overlap region; 0.268″ native beam ≈ 30 pc at the Antennae's distance, 1.33 km/s channels, 0.975 mJy/beam per 10 km/s, continuum sensitivity 0.030 mJy/beam). Archive QA2 cubes first; CASA re-imaging only if the QA2 products prove unusable for the two faint shock lines. No new observations, no external catalogs, surveys, or papers.

**Question.** The proposal's full aim — a pre-cluster-cloud (PCC) population with chemical formation timescales — is paper-length. This thesis takes its measurable core:

1. Among dense clumps above a fixed column-density cut, lying inside the region of the mosaic where the SiO limit reaches a fixed uniform depth, **what fraction show SiO(5–4) or HNCO(10₀,₁₀–9₀,₉) emission?**
2. For the (probably majority) non-detections, **where in velocity does the ensemble-stacked shock signal sit relative to the bulk gas?** SiO from a cloud–cloud collision need not appear at the CO centroid; a shifted or broad-winged stacked profile is itself a statement about how the dense gas is being assembled.

**Pre-registered headline configuration (single, fixed before analysis).** ¹³CO(2–1) catalog, 0.5″ uv-tapered cube. Every headline number is quoted from this one configuration. The native-0.27″ version and a C¹⁸O-based cross-check appear once, in a single appendix robustness table, and nowhere else.

**Work plan (one student, ~nine months).**

1. *Week 1 — literature check.* Confirm no published SiO/HNCO census exists from this OUS. The census + velocity-resolved stack survives either way.

2. *Months 1–2 — cubes, hard handoff date.* Line IDs verified in Splatalogue. Products: SiO(5–4) ~216.06 GHz, HNCO ~218.62, C¹⁸O(2–1) ~218.38, ¹³CO(2–1) ~219.22, H₂CO(3₀,₃–2₀,₂)/CH₃OH ~217.1–217.3 GHz, plus a 1.3 mm continuum image from the 231–235 GHz windows. Headline cubes are uv-tapered to 0.5″ (≈55 pc, the PCC scale the proposal targeted); the expected brightness-sensitivity gain over native resolution is ~2×, not more, since down-weighting long baselines raises per-beam noise, and the sample-depth cut in step 4 is built on the measured noise map, not on a predicted factor. Month 3 begins on whatever cubes exist on the handoff date.

3. *Month 3 — one clump catalog.* Dendrogram/CPROPS-style segmentation on the **¹³CO(2–1) 0.5″ cube** — the only line reliably above the noise at clump scales. C¹⁸O(2–1) supplies velocity centroids and an optical-depth check for the clumps where it is detected; it is not a segmentation input. Each entry carries position, deconvolved size, velocity centroid, linewidth, peak and integrated ¹³CO intensity, and a uv-filtering diagnostic (aperture flux vs. surrounding negative-bowl depth). Expect a few tens of objects.

4. *Month 4 — a denominator defined by physics.* The census sample is clumps whose ¹³CO-derived peak column exceeds a stated fixed cut **and** which lie inside the mosaic region where the SiO 3σ limit reaches a stated uniform depth, read off the per-pixel noise map. Everything else is excluded from the fraction and tabulated separately. This makes the fraction reproducible and, in principle, comparable to other datasets.

5. *Months 5–6 — measurement.* Aperture spectra of SiO and HNCO at every surviving position: an integrated intensity or a 3σ upper limit, each with the local noise attached.

6. *Months 7–9 — three results.*
   **(a) Column-selected detected fraction**, with binomial confidence intervals, always quoted in the same sentence as the column cut and the limiting depth.
   **(b) Velocity-resolved stack.** Clump spectra velocity-aligned to their C¹⁸O (else ¹³CO) centroids and co-added, with the stacking window wide enough to capture ±30 km/s offsets. The headline figure is **stacked SiO (and HNCO) intensity as a function of velocity offset from the centroid**, with the stacked S/N shown against integration-window width. Ratios are reported as *intensity* ratios I(SiO)/I(¹³CO), I(HNCO)/I(¹³CO) — not abundances, since one transition constrains no excitation. Secondary split: linewidth quartiles, as binned stacks only.
   **(c) Column-matched selectivity test.** Does grain/shock chemistry track something other than column? Clumps are split into H₂CO-bright and H₂CO-faint halves **at fixed ¹³CO integrated intensity** — i.e. matched in bins of I(¹³CO), so the comparison is between clumps of the same column — and the stacked SiO/¹³CO ratio compared between the matched samples. The discriminating quantity is stated in advance: an elevated **I(H₂CO)/I(¹³CO)** ratio, not raw H₂CO brightness, is what counts as "grain chemistry"; a threshold in that ratio is fixed before the SiO stacks are examined. If the H₂CO-bright and H₂CO-faint samples turn out not to overlap in column, the test is reported as inconclusive rather than forced.

**Cut deliberately, and stated as such:** no second clump catalog, no survival analysis (the censoring level scales as SiO noise / ¹³CO intensity, i.e. with the very covariate of interest, so a Kaplan–Meier ratio distribution would be biased in an unsignable direction), and no shock-peak/clump-centroid offset statistics (with the expected handful of detections, any such test is powerless; individual detections are presented as case studies with no statistical claim). Also out of scope: shock-model grids, chemical clocks, PCC formation timescales, non-LTE radiative transfer, and any global gas-mass budget, which single-configuration 12 m filtering makes indefensible. The 1.3 mm continuum is used only to flag clumps as possibly hosting embedded HII regions, since Band 6 alone cannot separate dust from free-free. Optional stretch, time permitting: LTE column densities for the brightest few SiO clumps across an assumed T_ex range, with the assumption's effect displayed.

**Deliverables.** The 0.5″ cubes and moment maps, one machine-readable ¹³CO clump catalog with filtering flags and SiO/HNCO intensities-or-limits, three figures (detected fraction with binomial errors; stacked SiO/HNCO versus velocity offset; column-matched H₂CO-bright vs. H₂CO-faint SiO/¹³CO comparison), and a one-page appendix robustness table repeating the fraction for the native-resolution and C¹⁸O variants.

**Why it is tractable.** Public, calibrated, modest-volume data; archive-first imaging bounds the schedule risk; standard CASA and Python tooling throughout; one pre-registered configuration means one headline number rather than four. The design produces a quotable result about a canonical merger in the most likely outcome — SiO faint across most of the overlap region — because a measured fraction, a stacked limit resolved in velocity, and a column-matched chemistry comparison are all measurements, not detections.
