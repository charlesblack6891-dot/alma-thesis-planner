**A quasi-simultaneous millimeter-to-submillimeter spectrum of the Sombrero galaxy's nucleus: an error-budget-first measurement of the spectral shape of an extremely sub-Eddington supermassive black hole**

Using only the archival ALMA Cycle 0 dataset 2011.0.00754.S — point-source continuum photometry of the M104 nucleus at up to 15 frequencies across Bands 3, 6, 7, and 9 — the student will measure the nuclear millimeter/submillimeter spectral shape and confront it with the two families of low-luminosity AGN SED models: ADAF-type hot accretion flows (flat-to-inverted, rising-then-peaking synchrotron spectrum) versus jet-dominated emission. The discrimination logic is stated asymmetrically and honestly from the outset: a *steep* measured slope (α ≲ −0.5, S_ν ∝ ν^α) is decisive — it rules out ADAF dominance and indicates optically thin jet emission — whereas a flat-to-inverted slope is consistent with both an ADAF and a compact, partially self-absorbed jet core, in which case the thesis reports a constrained-but-degenerate outcome and quantifies what additional spectral information (curvature, peak frequency) would break the degeneracy. Both outcomes are physically meaningful thesis conclusions; neither is a failure branch.

The project is front-loaded with two gating milestones before any science claim is made. **Milestone 1 (weeks 1–2): archive, epoch, and software inventory.** Query the archive to establish (a) which of the 15 frequency settings were actually executed and calibratable — Cycle 0 programs were often partially observed — and (b) the actual execution date of every scheduling block, per frequency setting. Because quasi-simultaneity is the dataset's central scientific advantage and the source is a known millimeter variable, the thesis will tabulate the epoch spread explicitly: settings observed within days of each other are treated as one epoch; any setting executed weeks or months apart is flagged, and the achievable variability systematic is folded into that frequency's error bar rather than assumed away. The baseline science plan assumes a Bands 3–7 (~100–350 GHz) spectrum; Band 9, the most likely casualty, is a bonus extension to ~700 GHz, not a requirement. The milestone also resolves the named software hazard: the original reduction scripts target CASA 3.x/4.x, so the plan commits in week 1 to either archive-delivered calibrated measurement sets or a pinned legacy CASA environment, with porting to modern CASA attempted only if both fail. **Milestone 2 (weeks 2–4): quantitative calibration error budget for both slope and curvature.** Using Cycle 0 absolute-calibration accuracies (~5% at Band 3, ~10% at Band 7, 10–20% at Band 9), propagate cross-band flux-scale systematics into the achievable uncertainty on the spectral index itself — approximately σ(α) ≈ ±0.1 over the 100–350 GHz lever arm, which is explicitly sufficient for the steep-versus-flat test — and separately compute the minimum detectable spectral curvature. If the budget shows curvature is undetectable at these systematics, the curvature deliverable is reframed in advance as an upper-limit/consistency statement, and the science case rests on the slope, whose adequacy the same budget has just demonstrated.

**Core reduction (the bulk of the semester):** manual calibration of pre-pipeline Cycle 0 data via the Milestone-1 pathway, budgeted as a multi-week task. Nuclear flux densities at each frequency are extracted by visibility-plane point-source fitting, appropriate for an unresolved ~100 mJy core with little contamination. If Band 9 data exist, phase decoherence biases point-source amplitudes low; self-calibration on the bright core is the planned correction, with the pre/post self-cal amplitude difference reported in the error budget — and if self-calibration fails to converge at Cycle 0 sensitivity, Band 9 is dropped with no loss of the central claims.

**Deliverables, in guaranteed-to-ambitious order:** (1) a reproducible reduction and an error-budgeted table of nuclear flux densities, with per-frequency epochs and the verified simultaneity spread reported alongside; (2) the millimeter spectral index α with the Milestone-2 systematic error bar, plus a statistically defined test for curvature/turnover interpreted strictly within that budget; (3) the model-family confrontation under the asymmetric logic above — a decisive statement if the slope is steep, an honest degeneracy analysis (ADAF versus self-absorbed jet core) if it is flat, in either case at the qualitative level only, with no radiative-transfer SED fitting or accretion-parameter derivation. All work uses the named ALMA dataset plus standard tools (CASA, Python); no external catalogs or literature photometry are load-bearing anywhere in the plan.
