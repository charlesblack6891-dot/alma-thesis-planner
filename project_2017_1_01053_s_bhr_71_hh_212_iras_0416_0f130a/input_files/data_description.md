## Proposal abstract

Prior observations of molecular outflows have revealed that not only do these harsh, energetic environments contain a surprising array of complex molecules, but they also show highly-localized spatio-chemical differentiation. Because the velocities of these jets are relatively well-constrained, it is possible to associate distance within the outflow with temporal evolution of chemistry. As well, the collimated nature of the outflows provides a relatively compact region in which comparisons can be made between outflow, shocked walls, and background ambient gas in a variety of density and temperature conditions as the chemistry evolves. Here, we propose to conduct a broad, unbiased Band 7 spectral line survey with the ACA of seven southern outflows of widely-varying ages, velocities, and chemical conditions to elucidate the underlying links between physical conditions, outflow properties, and chemical evolution in these important pre-stellar environment.

### Likely spectral lines in the coverage

*These are nearby Galactic protostellar/outflow sources (BHR 71, HH 212, HH 46, L1551 IRS 5, IRAS 04166+2706, CG 30, G331.5-0.1), all within a few hundred pc, so no redshift correction is needed — rest frequencies are used directly.*

- **SiO J=8–7, 347.331 GHz** (in 345.2–349.1 GHz windows) — companion shock tracer, higher critical density
- **¹²CO J=3–2, 345.796 GHz** (in 345.1–347.2 GHz windows) — bulk entrained outflow gas, cavity walls, ambient cloud
- **SO N,J=8,7–7,6, 304.078 GHz** (in 302.0–304.0 GHz) and **SO 8₈–7₇, 344.311 GHz** (in 343.3–345.3 GHz) — shock-enhanced sulfur chemistry

### Scientific context

This unbiased Band 7 ACA line survey targets a set of well-studied, chemically rich protostellar outflows spanning a range of dynamical ages, so the frequency setup was clearly chosen to bracket the most diagnostic shock and dense-gas tracers rather than a single species. SiO and SO/SO₂ trace sputtering and grain destruction in fast, dissociative J-shocks along the jet axis, while CO and its isotopologues map the swept-up, entrained molecular cavity and its kinematics; comparing their relative spatial offsets lets one reconstruct the outflow's shock history and age gradient. CH₃OH and other complex organics probe the sublimation of ice mantles in lower-velocity C-shocks at the cavity walls, directly testing the "spatio-chemical differentiation" hypothesis in the proposal — that different chemical families mark different physical zones (jet core, shocked shell, quiescent envelope) rather than a single well-mixed reservoir. The inclusion of deuterated species (N₂D⁺, DCO⁺) alongside their hydrogenated counterparts gives a chemical-clock diagnostic sensitive to the pre-shock gas history, complementing the purely kinematic age indicators from line widths and velocity offsets. With ~1 km/s velocity resolution and 3–4″ angular resolution (tens to a few thousand AU at these distances), the ACA data can resolve velocity-structured outflow lobes and compare abundance ratios between outflow, shocked wall, and ambient gas across all seven sources on a common footing, while the modest continuum sensitivity (~0.3–0.7 mJy/bm) mainly supports source/driving-position identification rather than detailed dust modeling.
