## Proposal abstract

We propose to map in the 12CO(1-0) line a region of 6'x4' in the south-west of the galaxy NGC 4254, outside its optical disc, and characterised by several compact regions of star formation. These HII regions, detected in very deep narrow-band Halpha images obtained during the complete Virgo cluster survey VESTIGE, have been formed after a rapid flyby encounter of this massive galaxy with another Virgo cluster member. The analysis of the stellar populations of these compact regions done using SED fitting codes based on a unique set of multifrequency data, from the far-UV (GALEX) to the far-IR (Herschel), indicate that star formation has abruptely ceased in some of them. The molecular gas data will be used to study the relationship between gas column density and star formation activity in extreme environments (cold gas clouds surrounded by a hot intracluster medium) with the purpose of understanding the fate of the stripped material in high density regions. This analysis will shade light on the origin of the diffuse intracluster light observed in clusters, as well as on that of compact unbound sources such as ultra compact dwarf galaxies.

## Scientific context (LLM annotation)

### Likely spectral lines in the coverage

- **¹²CO(1–0), 115.2712 GHz (rest)** — redshifted to ≈114.35 GHz, falling squarely in the 113.9–114.8 GHz window. This is the explicitly targeted line (assumed systemic velocity ~2407 km/s, z≈0.00803, consistent with NGC 4254/M99).
- **CN N=1–0 hyperfine multiplet, ≈113.123–113.509 GHz (rest)** — redshifted to ≈112.19–112.55 GHz, within the 111.3–113.3 GHz window. A PDR/diffuse-cloud tracer sensitive to UV field and gas density, likely present as a serendipitous, comparatively faint line alongside the CO setup.
- **H38α radio recombination line, ≈115.266 GHz (rest)** — redshifted to essentially the same observed frequency as ¹²CO(1–0) in the 113.9–114.8 GHz window. Traces ionized gas in HII regions; usually far too faint to detect in individual compact knots at this distance, but worth flagging as a potential (minor) blend/contaminant with the CO line.
- **HC3N J=11–10, 100.0764 GHz (rest)** — redshifted to ≈99.28 GHz, within the 99.2–101.2 GHz window. Dense-gas tracer; likely non-detected in these diffuse, low-metallicity tidal-debris clouds but included in coverage.
- **CH3CCH J=6(0)–5(0), 102.5480 GHz (rest)** — redshifted to ≈101.73 GHz, within the 101.2–103.2 GHz window. A weak gas-temperature/density diagnostic typically associated with hot-core-type chemistry; unlikely to be robustly detected in this low-density, stripped-gas environment but nominally in band.

### Scientific context

The Band 3 setup is built around ¹²CO(1–0) as a direct tracer of the bulk molecular gas reservoir in HII-region complexes embedded in the ram-pressure/tidal debris south-west of NGC 4254, enabling gas surface density measurements to be compared pixel-by-pixel with the UV–far-IR-derived star-formation histories from VESTIGE and SED fitting. The auxiliary windows nominally cover CN, HC3N, and CH3CCH, species that in normal galaxy disks trace photodissociation-region chemistry and denser, warmer gas phases, respectively; given the low expected column densities and diffuse nature of stripped intracluster gas, these are more plausible as sensitive upper limits than firm detections, but their presence in the correlator setup allows a check on whether any surviving clouds have unusually dense or shielded interiors. The near-coincidence of the H38α recombination line with the redshifted CO(1–0) frequency is a known hazard for Galactic-disk CO(1–0) surveys and should be checked for even though free-free emission from these low-mass HII regions is expected to be faint. The achieved continuum sensitivity (~0.043 mJy/bm) mainly constrains dust/free-free continuum non-detections rather than yielding significant photometry, given the modest expected dust masses of these small, likely low-metallicity clumps. Combined with the sub-2″ angular resolution (a few hundred pc at the Virgo distance) and ~1.5 km/s velocity resolution, the data can resolve individual compact star-forming clumps kinematically and morphologically, testing whether the abrupt quenching inferred from SED fitting corresponds to gas removal, gas exhaustion, or a decoupling of molecular gas from the ionized/UV-traced star formation — directly bearing on the fate of stripped material as a possible source of intracluster light or ultra-compact dwarf galaxy progenitors.
