**Title:** Mapping Shock Chemistry Along the HOPS 315 Jet: SiO/CO Intensity-Ratio Evolution and Outflow Energetics from ALMA Line Cubes

**Description:** Using only the existing ALMASOP Band 6 CO(2-1) and SiO(5-4) cubes for HOPS 315 (no new data, no external models), the student produces a self-contained, two-chapter thesis entirely within the ALMA dataset.

**Chapter 0 (scoping, done first):** Read Dutta et al. (2020) to check whether CO-based outflow mass/momentum/mass-loss-rate values are already published for this source. If so, cite and build forward from them rather than re-deriving as novel; if not, perform standard moment-0/1/8 maps and position-velocity diagrams in CO and SiO to establish lobe extent, position angle, and inclination. This fixes the geometric/kinematic framework needed for Chapter 1 and is reported as scaffolding.

**Chapter 1 — CO-based outflow energetics with an honest uncertainty budget:** Compute outflow mass, momentum, characteristic velocity, dynamical timescale, mass outflow rate, and mechanical luminosity from ¹²CO(2-1) using standard optically-thin LTE methodology (Bally/Arce/Dunham-style), reporting results as a range over a realistic excitation-temperature grid (20–100 K) and inclination uncertainty, with the standard optically-thin-assumption caveat stated plainly (no borrowed external opacity-correction factors). This is a bounded, well-precedented technical exercise standing on its own.

**Chapter 2 — SiO/CO intensity-ratio profile as a shock-processing diagnostic (the thesis's real contribution):** Using the two already-mapped, matched-resolution cubes, construct a spatial profile of the SiO(5-4)/CO(2-1) integrated-intensity ratio (and, where S/N allows, an LTE column-density ratio) along the jet axis from the protostar outward through both lobes. Because SiO is released into the gas phase primarily by grain-mantle sputtering in shocks and re-freezes onto grains on a timescale of ~10²–10³ yr, this ratio is expected to peak near shock sites (e.g., SiO knots) and decline with distance/dynamical age as processed gas ages away from the driving shock, while CO traces the swept-up cavity throughout at roughly constant relative abundance. The student measures where the ratio peaks and how it declines outward in each lobe, checks whether the decline is smooth or knot-associated (tying it to the SiO clump positions already visible in the Chapter 0 PV diagrams), and compares the blue vs. red lobe profiles for asymmetry. This produces a genuine, self-contained result — a shock-chemistry aging sequence along one resolved protostellar jet — using an established technique from the Podio/Lefloch/Gusdorf-style shock-tracer literature, without invoking any entrainment model, timescale-reconciliation argument, or numbers external to the two provided ALMA cubes.

This design keeps the two chapters tightly scoped (geometry + energetics; SiO/CO ratio profile), avoids any dependence on the JWST-derived hot-jet numbers or unconstrained entrainment modeling, and is achievable in one semester to a year using standard CASA moment-map, PV-diagram, and LTE ratio-analysis techniques applied only to the described CO(2-1) and SiO(5-4) cubes.
