**Software environment and data preparation**

The student works entirely within CASA (version 6.5.4, matching the pipeline calibration version used for these data, to avoid tool-chain inconsistencies) plus standard Python analysis packages (NumPy, SciPy, Astropy, Matplotlib, and spectral-cube for cube manipulation). The two pipeline-calibrated measurement sets / image cubes — ¹²CO(2–1) at 230.53800 GHz and SiO(5–4) at 217.10498 GHz, both imaged with robust = +2.0 — are the sole data inputs. The student begins by loading both cubes in CASA (`imstat`, `imhead`) to confirm pixel scale, spectral axis (converting channel number to LSRK velocity using the rest frequencies above), beam size (~0.46"×0.40" for SiO, ~0.42"×0.36" for CO), and RMS noise per channel (measured in line-free channels via `imstat` over an off-source region). Because the two cubes have slightly different native resolutions, the student uses `imsmooth` to convolve the CO cube to the (slightly larger) SiO beam, producing a matched-resolution pair — this common-beam version is what all ratio work in Chapter 2 will use, while the native-resolution CO cube is retained for Chapter 1 energetics (where beam-matching is not required).

**Chapter 0 — Literature check and geometric/kinematic scaffolding**

Before any independent analysis, the student reads Dutta et al. (2020) specifically to extract any already-published CO-based outflow mass, momentum, mass-loss rate, dynamical timescale, position angle, inclination, and lobe extent for HOPS 315. These values are tabulated and cited directly rather than re-derived as a "new" result. Regardless of what is already published, the student independently reproduces the basic geometric maps needed as a working foundation for Chapters 1 and 2:

- Moment-0 (integrated intensity), moment-1 (intensity-weighted velocity), and moment-8 (peak intensity) maps in both CO and SiO, generated in CASA with `immoments`, using a consistent velocity range and a clipping mask based on a signal-to-noise threshold (e.g., 3σ, with σ from the line-free channel RMS) to exclude noise-dominated pixels.
- Blue- and red-shifted lobe masks defined by integrating moment-0 over blueshifted and redshifted velocity ranges relative to the source systemic velocity (taken from the literature value or estimated from the compact SiO/CO emission nearest the protostar).
- A position–velocity (PV) diagram extracted along the jet axis (position angle determined from the moment-0 morphology) in both CO and SiO, produced with CASA's `impv` task, to establish jet extent, terminal velocities, and the location of discrete velocity/spatial knots (particularly in SiO, which is expected to appear knotty rather than smooth).
- From the moment-0 maps, the student measures projected lobe length (protostar to lobe tip) and, combined with an inclination estimate (from the literature, or from the ratio of proper-motion-free morphological opening if not otherwise available), estimates deprojected length and dynamical timescale (t_dyn = length / characteristic velocity) for each lobe.

This step is reported as scaffolding rather than a novel result: its purpose is to fix the geometric and kinematic reference frame (axis, position angle, systemic velocity, lobe boundaries, knot locations) used consistently in both later chapters.

**Chapter 1 — CO-based outflow energetics with an explicit uncertainty budget**

Using the native-resolution ¹²CO(2–1) cube, the student computes standard outflow physical quantities under the optically-thin, LTE approximation (following the standard Bally/Arce/Dunham-family method used throughout the low-mass outflow literature):

1. Convert the CO(2–1) brightness temperature cube to column density per velocity channel assuming LTE and a single excitation temperature T_ex, using the standard partition-function and Einstein-A-coefficient expressions for the J=2–1 transition, adopting the CO/H₂ abundance ratio standard in this literature (~10⁻⁴).
2. Mask each channel at the same 3σ threshold used in Chapter 0, and restrict the integration to velocities identified as outflow (i.e., excluding the ambient cloud/systemic velocity range contaminated by extended emission).
3. Sum column density over the masked region and convert to total gas mass per lobe, then compute mass-weighted mean velocity, momentum (M×v), and characteristic velocity for each lobe.
4. Using the dynamical timescale from Chapter 0, derive mass outflow rate (M/t_dyn) and mechanical luminosity (½Mv²/t_dyn) per lobe.
5. Repeat steps 1–4 over a grid of excitation temperatures spanning 20–100 K (a standard assumed range for entrained molecular outflow gas), and separately propagate inclination uncertainty (by varying the assumed inclination angle within a plausible range) through the deprojected velocity and length terms. Report all derived quantities as ranges (or tabulated at a few representative T_ex values, e.g., 20, 50, 100 K) rather than single point estimates.
6. State explicitly, as a caveat in the results (not in a "limitations" section but as part of the standard method description), that these values assume optically thin ¹²CO emission and do not include any opacity correction from an optically thick tracer — consistent with using only the two cubes provided.

Where Chapter 0's literature check finds directly comparable published values, the student compares their independently derived range against the literature number as a consistency/validation check on the pipeline (do the reproduced numbers fall within the published range, given the stated T_ex and inclination uncertainty).

**Chapter 2 — SiO/CO intensity-ratio profile as a shock-chemistry diagnostic**

This chapter uses the common-beam (SiO-resolution-matched) CO and SiO moment-0 maps from the data-preparation step.

1. Define a spatial ordinate along the jet axis (the position angle fixed in Chapter 0), and extract 1-D integrated-intensity profiles of both CO(2–1) and SiO(5–4) by averaging moment-0 flux in the matched-resolution maps within a series of contiguous apertures (sized to roughly the beam width) stepped outward from the protostar through each lobe. This produces a discrete sequence of (projected distance, I_CO, I_SiO) points per lobe.
2. Compute the SiO/CO integrated-intensity ratio at each aperture position, propagating uncertainty from the per-channel RMS noise (via standard error propagation through the moment-0 summation) to get an error bar on each ratio point.
3. Where signal-to-noise permits (i.e., where both lines are securely detected, typically nearer the source and at known SiO knot positions), convert the intensity ratio to an LTE column-density ratio N(SiO)/N(CO) using the same excitation-temperature assumption approach as Chapter 1 (a single representative T_ex or a small grid), to give a more physically interpretable version of the same profile; where S/N is insufficient for SiO detection, the student instead reports upper limits on the ratio from the 3σ SiO noise.
4. Overlay the resulting ratio profile (both intensity-ratio and, where available, column-density-ratio versions) on the PV diagram and moment-0 maps from Chapter 0 to identify whether ratio peaks coincide spatially with the discrete SiO knots already visible in the moment-8/PV data, or instead track a smooth outward decline.
5. Fit (or simply characterize, e.g., via a piecewise or exponential-like trend by eye and a simple linear/exponential regression in log-ratio vs. distance space) the decline of the ratio with projected distance from the protostar in each lobe separately, and quantify a characteristic length scale over which the ratio falls by, e.g., a factor of 2, in each lobe.
6. Compare the blue-lobe and red-lobe profiles directly (peak ratio value, location of the peak, and decline length scale) to assess lobe-to-lobe asymmetry, cross-referencing with the independently derived lobe dynamical timescales from Chapter 1 to discuss the ratio trend as a function of estimated age since the driving shock event (using only the timescales already derived from the CO data in Chapter 1 — no external entrainment or chemical-model timescales are introduced).
7. Cross-check whether ratio peak locations correspond to any localized velocity structure (e.g., high-velocity SiO knots) evident in the PV diagrams, to assess whether elevated SiO/CO is spatially discrete (shock-knot-associated) or continuous along the outflow.

All quantitative results in both chapters are presented with explicit propagated uncertainties (from noise, T_ex assumption, and inclination where relevant) and are derived solely from the two provided ALMA Band 6 cubes, using standard, well-precedented CASA imaging/analysis tasks (`immoments`, `impv`, `imsmooth`, `imstat`) and LTE column-density formulae applied consistently across both chapters.
