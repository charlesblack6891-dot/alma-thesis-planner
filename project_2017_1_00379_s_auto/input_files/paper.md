## Abstract

Galaxy mergers can drive dense molecular gas into large-scale outflows, but whether this outflowing gas is dynamically compressed in a way that promotes star formation, rather than simply being dispersed or heated by shocks and AGN-related processes, remains poorly constrained observationally, particularly on the clump scales where star formation would actually proceed. NGC 3256, a late-stage luminous infrared merger hosting a well-documented molecular outflow, offers a case study for this question, and the HCN(3–2)/HCO+(3–2) line ratio provides an accessible proxy for the chemistry and excitation conditions of the gas that can be compared between outflowing and disk material without requiring a full multi-line density analysis. This work uses archival Band 6 ALMA data from a single project (2017.1.00379.S) that simultaneously covers both lines at three angular resolutions (4.352″, 0.8″, and 0.237″), re-imaged from the calibrated visibilities with matched parameters so that the two lines share an identical beam within each dataset. At the two coarser resolutions, matched-mask moment-0 maps and position–velocity diagrams will be used to separate outflow- from disk-associated emission using fixed, literature-motivated velocity and spatial criteria, and the HCN/HCO+ ratio will be compared between these two components at each resolution independently, testing whether the outflow shows a distinguishable ratio enhancement and whether any such signal is consistent across resolutions. At the highest resolution, several discrete clumps within the outflow-velocity gas will be identified and characterized through two-dimensional Gaussian fits to their sizes (reported as upper limits where unresolved) and one-dimensional fits to their linewidths, yielding order-of-magnitude virial mass estimates and, where possible, a size–linewidth relation for comparison to established Galactic and extragalactic trends; local line ratios at these clump positions will then be compared to the global outflow-versus-disk contrast to assess whether ratio enhancement coincides with the compact, marginally resolved clumps expected under a compression scenario. Throughout, the line ratio is treated explicitly as a first-order chemical and kinematic diagnostic rather than a substitute for a full density and temperature analysis, and the study is designed to establish whether the observed spatial, kinematic, and clump-scale distribution of the HCN/HCO+ ratio in NGC 3256 is consistent with compression-driven chemistry in the outflow, providing a complementary observational test to more detailed excitation modeling of this system.

## 1. Introduction

Mergers between gas-rich galaxies are among the most efficient mechanisms for redistributing molecular gas on galactic scales, and a growing body of observational work has shown that this redistribution can take the form of large-scale outflows in which dense, star-formation-tracing gas is entrained and driven out of the nuclear region rather than simply consumed in situ. These outflows are usually invoked as a negative feedback mechanism, one that removes fuel for future star formation and helps regulate the growth of the host galaxy and its central black hole. However, an outflow's net effect on star formation is not obviously one-directional. Gas that is swept up and dynamically compressed as it is accelerated out of the disk could, in principle, be driven to higher densities in the process, creating localized conditions that favor rather than suppress star formation within the outflowing material itself. Distinguishing between these two qualitatively different outcomes — dispersal and heating that suppress star formation, versus compression that promotes it — requires observational diagnostics that are sensitive to the physical and chemical state of the gas, not just its kinematics or bulk mass.

This distinction is difficult to make in practice. Molecular outflows are diffuse and comparatively faint relative to the disk gas from which they originate, so most studies to date have characterized them primarily through their kinematics and mass-outflow rates, using tracers and resolutions that reveal where the gas is going and how fast, but not necessarily what physical or chemical processes are acting on it once it gets there. Excitation and chemistry are more direct proxies for the underlying density and radiation environment, but obtaining a robust, multi-transition constraint on density and temperature for outflowing gas — the kind of full radiative transfer modeling that could unambiguously establish compression — is often observationally expensive and may not always be feasible for the specific dataset in hand. There is a need for a diagnostic that is more accessible than a complete density-temperature analysis, while still being informative about whether the outflowing gas's chemical and excitation state differs meaningfully from that of the quiescent disk. Compounding this problem is a scale mismatch: the process of interest, gas compression that might trigger star formation, presumably operates at the scale of individual clumps within the outflow, but many outflow observations are made at resolutions too coarse to resolve any such structure, leaving open the question of whether any global chemical signature actually coincides with compact, dense sub-structures or is instead a smoothed-out artifact of beam-averaging over a mix of conditions.

The ratio of two commonly observed dense-gas tracers, HCN and HCO+, offers one route to a more accessible diagnostic. Because these two molecules respond differently to ionization, shocks, and UV or X-ray irradiation, their line ratio has long been used as an indicator of the chemical and excitation environment of dense gas, without requiring the assumptions or line coverage needed for a full non-LTE density analysis. Applied differentially, comparing the ratio in outflowing gas to that in the disk of the same galaxy, it becomes a tool for asking whether the outflowing gas occupies a distinguishable chemical regime, one that could plausibly be linked to compression, while explicitly not attempting to derive absolute densities or temperatures from the ratio alone.

NGC 3256 is a well-suited target for this kind of test. It is a late-stage luminous infrared merger with a molecular outflow that has already been characterized kinematically in the literature, providing an established framework for defining outflow-associated velocities and geometry against which any new chemical measurement can be interpreted. Archival ALMA Band 6 observations of this source (project 2017.1.00379.S) cover both HCN(3–2) and HCO+(3–2) simultaneously, at three separate angular resolutions ranging from a few arcseconds down to a small fraction of an arcsecond. Because both lines are observed in the same tuning and same execution at each resolution, they share an identical synthesized beam within each dataset, which removes the need for cross-line convolution or regridding and allows the ratio to be computed directly and consistently at each of the three scales.

This study uses that archival dataset to test, at multiple physical scales, whether the HCN/HCO+(3–2) ratio in NGC 3256's outflow is distinguishable from that of the disk, and whether any such distinction is associated with compact, marginally resolved clumps consistent with a compression scenario. At the two coarser resolutions, moment-0 maps constructed from a shared detection mask will be used, together with position–velocity diagrams and literature-motivated velocity and spatial criteria, to separate outflow-associated emission from disk emission, and the ratio will be compared between the two components independently at each resolution, providing an internal check on whether any signal is robust to the choice of angular scale rather than being an artifact of a single dataset. At the highest resolution, individual clumps within the outflow-velocity gas will be identified and characterized through two-dimensional size fits and one-dimensional linewidth fits, yielding order-of-magnitude virial mass estimates, and, where enough clumps yield resolved sizes, a size–linewidth relation that can be compared qualitatively to established trends from Galactic and extragalactic molecular clouds. The local line ratio at each clump position will then be compared to the global outflow-versus-disk contrast established at coarser resolution, to assess whether any ratio enhancement is spatially coincident with these compact structures rather than being distributed more broadly across the outflow.

Because the HCN/HCO+ ratio alone cannot uniquely establish density or temperature, this study is explicit that its result is a first-order chemical and kinematic diagnostic rather than a substitute for full multi-line excitation modeling. Its success or failure will be assessed by whether the ratio shows a statistically distinguishable contrast between outflow and disk gas that is consistent across independently analyzed resolutions, and by whether that contrast, where present, coincides spatially with the compact clumps identified at the highest resolution, rather than by any presumption of what that outcome will be. In this way, the analysis is designed to provide an observationally grounded, complementary test of the compression-driven star formation hypothesis in NGC 3256's outflow, one that can stand alongside, and be checked against, more detailed density and excitation modeling of the same system.

## 2. Methods

### Overview

The analysis plan uses a single archival ALMA project to construct a self-consistent, multi-scale test of whether outflowing molecular gas in NGC 3256 shows a chemically distinguishable HCN(3–2)/HCO+(3–2) line ratio relative to disk gas, and whether any such distinction is spatially coincident with compact, marginally resolved clumps. The approach proceeds in three stages that mirror the scientific logic laid out above: (1) preparation of matched-beam image cubes for both lines at three angular resolutions; (2) a two-resolution comparison of the line ratio between kinematically and spatially defined outflow and disk regions; and (3) a highest-resolution clump-scale analysis that connects local ratio behavior to clump sizes, linewidths, and virial masses. Throughout, the HCN/HCO+ ratio is treated as a first-order chemical and kinematic diagnostic — informative about relative ionization, shock, and irradiation conditions — rather than as a substitute for a full non-LTE density and temperature analysis.

### Dataset

The dataset consists exclusively of archival ALMA Band 6 observations from project 2017.1.00379.S, queried through the ALMA Science Archive. Only the three member OUS (MOUS) products relevant to the two target lines will be retrieved: `uid://A001/X1273/Xb4b` (angular resolution 0.237″), `uid://A001/X1273/Xb4d` (0.8″), and `uid://A001/X1273/Xb4f` (4.352″). All three MOUS cover the same four spectral windows (249.0–250.8, 250.7–252.6, 262.8–264.7, and 264.6–266.4 GHz), so HCN(3–2) (rest frequency 265.886 GHz, observed at approximately 263.4 GHz) and HCO+(3–2) (rest frequency 267.558 GHz, observed at approximately 265.1 GHz) are both present in every dataset. For each MOUS, the pipeline-calibrated measurement sets and the delivered QA2 image cubes will be downloaded.

Before any imaging or analysis, the line identifications will be verified against the Splatalogue database, confirming rest frequencies and checking for possible line blending within the ±5 km/s channel width given NGC 3256's systemic redshift of z ≈ 0.0093. This step establishes that the two target transitions are cleanly separated and correctly identified in each spectral window prior to imaging.

### Step 1: Re-imaging the calibrated visibilities

Rather than relying solely on the delivered QA2 cubes, the calibrated visibilities for each of the three MOUS will be re-imaged directly in CASA, using the CASA pipeline version indicated in the corresponding QA2 weblog for that MOUS. For each MOUS, `tclean` will be re-run on the two spectral windows containing HCN(3–2) and HCO+(3–2), applying consistent imaging parameters across the two lines within a given MOUS: Briggs weighting with a fixed robust parameter, a common pixel size set relative to the synthesized beam, and a matched velocity channel width of 4.424 km/s as delivered. Because both lines are observed in the same tuning and execution, imaging them identically within each MOUS guarantees that the resulting cubes for HCN and HCO+ share a common synthesized beam and channelization, removing the need for any subsequent cross-line convolution or spatial regridding when computing ratios. The pipeline's standard primary-beam correction will be applied to each cube.

Each re-imaged cube will be inspected for the spatial and spectral extent of line emission, and the noise level in line-free channels will be checked against the archive-quoted sensitivity (approximately 0.5–6.3 mJy/beam, depending on resolution) as a quality-control step before proceeding to scientific analysis.

### Step 2: Moment maps and matched-beam ratio construction (4.352″ and 0.8″ cubes)

At each of the two coarser resolutions, independently, the RMS noise will first be estimated per channel from line-free regions of the cube. A masked moment-0 (integrated intensity) map of HCN(3–2) will then be generated using a fixed per-channel signal-to-noise threshold (e.g., 3σ), summing over channels showing contiguous signal following a standard masked-moment approach: CASA's `immoments` task will be used with a mask derived from a smoothed, thresholded version of the cube, chosen specifically to avoid the noise bias that arises from summing over all channels without a detection mask.

The identical spatial mask derived from the HCN map will then be applied to the HCO+ cube at the same channels, producing a matched moment-0 map for HCO+ evaluated over exactly the same pixel and velocity footprint as the HCN map. Because the two lines share an identical beam within each MOUS by construction (Step 1), no additional convolution or regridding between lines is required at this stage.

The HCN moment-0 map will be divided by the HCO+ moment-0 map on a pixel-by-pixel basis, restricted to pixels where both maps exceed the shared noise-based detection cutoff; all other pixels will be masked (blanked) rather than used to extrapolate a ratio from a non-detection. Flux uncertainties will be propagated into the ratio using standard error-propagation formulas for the ratio of two noisy quantities, yielding a companion ratio-uncertainty map alongside each ratio map. This procedure will be carried out independently for the 4.352″ and 0.8″ datasets, producing two independent matched-beam ratio maps and associated uncertainty maps.

### Step 3: Kinematic separation of outflow and disk emission

For each of the two coarser-resolution HCN(3–2) cubes, an intensity-weighted mean velocity field (moment-1 map) will be computed using the same detection mask as in Step 2. The large-scale kinematic (merger/disk) axis will be identified directly from this observed velocity field rather than from a fitted rotation model. A position–velocity (PV) diagram will then be constructed along this axis using CASA's `impv` task, generated separately for the HCN and HCO+ cubes at each resolution.

Outflow-associated emission will be defined operationally using two joint criteria applied directly to the cube: (a) velocity channels offset from the systemic velocity (approximately 2800 km/s) by more than a fixed threshold, set using the PV diagram and following the velocity offset adopted in the PI's proposal and prior published outflow work on NGC 3256; and (b) spatial pixels offset from the main emission ridge or disk structure identified in the moment-0 map. Pixels satisfying both criteria will be flagged as "outflow"; pixels near systemic velocity and coincident with the main ridge will be flagged as "disk." This masking will be applied separately to the 4.352″ and 0.8″ cubes, using the same fixed velocity and spatial thresholds at both resolutions so that any comparison between them constitutes an internal consistency check rather than a re-tuned analysis.

### Step 4: Ratio comparison between disk and outflow gas

For each resolution independently, the mean and standard deviation of the pixel-by-pixel HCN/HCO+ ratio (from Step 2) will be computed separately within the outflow mask and within the disk mask (from Step 3). The two resulting pixel distributions will be compared using a two-sample statistical test appropriate for likely non-Gaussian pixel distributions, such as the Mann-Whitney U test, to assess whether the outflow gas shows a statistically distinguishable HCN/HCO+ ratio relative to the disk at each resolution. The ratio contrast (outflow mean divided by disk mean) and its associated uncertainty will be reported for both the 4.352″ and 0.8″ datasets, and the two independent resolutions will be checked against one another for consistency, providing an internal cross-check on the robustness of any detected signal.

This ratio contrast will be interpreted explicitly as a proxy for chemistry and excitation conditions — ionization balance, and shock- or AGN-driven chemistry versus gravitational-compression-driven chemistry — rather than as a direct density or kinetic-temperature measurement, and the analysis will state plainly whether the results do or do not support an enhanced HCN/HCO+ ratio in outflowing gas relative to disk gas. Separately, the spatial and velocity extent of the outflow-flagged emission will be compared qualitatively against the outflow geometry described in the PI's proposal and prior literature, with explicit consideration in the discussion of whether any velocity-offset emission is more plausibly tidal debris — a common feature of a late-stage merger such as NGC 3256 — rather than true outflowing gas. This comparison is qualitative and literature-based, not an independent kinematic decomposition.

### Step 5: Clump identification at highest resolution

Using the 0.237″ HCN(3–2) cube, attention will be restricted to velocity channels flagged as "outflow" under the same fixed velocity threshold applied in Step 3, reapplied at this resolution. Discrete, compact emission peaks ("clumps") that stand clearly above the local noise will be identified both visually and quantitatively, using peak signal-to-noise per channel or in the moment-0 map restricted to outflow velocities, targeting a sample of 3–5 clumps. For each candidate clump position, the corresponding HCO+ cube at the same resolution will be checked for co-located emission as a cross-check on the reality of the feature.

For each identified clump, a sub-cube centered on its position will be extracted for further analysis.

### Step 6: Clump size and linewidth fitting

For each clump sub-cube, a two-dimensional Gaussian will be fit to the spatial emission distribution in the moment-0 map (or in the peak channel) using CASA's `imfit` task, with HCN and HCO+ fit independently. The fitted Gaussian size will be deconvolved from the synthesized beam, using the deconvolved size and uncertainty reported directly by `imfit`. Given that the 0.237″ beam corresponds to a physical scale of tens of parsecs at the distance of NGC 3256, several clumps are expected to be only marginally resolved; in cases where the deconvolved FWHM is not statistically distinct from zero — that is, where the fitted size does not exceed the beam within its uncertainty — only an upper limit on the true source size will be reported, using the beam size itself as that upper limit.

For each clump, a one-dimensional spectral line profile will also be extracted at the clump position, spatially integrated over the fitted clump aperture, and a single Gaussian will be fit to obtain the velocity linewidth (FWHM) and its uncertainty. Where the profile is visibly non-Gaussian, this will be noted explicitly, and the FWHM will instead be measured directly from the profile.

### Step 7: Virial mass estimation and size–linewidth relation

Using the fitted (or upper-limit) sizes and linewidths from Step 6, an order-of-magnitude virial mass will be computed for each clump using the standard virial mass formula for a self-gravitating cloud, M_vir ≈ f × R × Δv², where R is the deconvolved (or upper-limit) clump radius, Δv is the fitted linewidth, and f is a standard geometry-dependent virial coefficient taken from the literature. Size uncertainties (or upper limits) will be explicitly propagated through to the resulting mass estimates. Given the marginal resolution of the beam relative to clump size, all masses will be reported as order-of-magnitude estimates; where only a size upper limit is available, the corresponding virial mass will be reported as a lower limit and presented as a valid quantitative result rather than as an indeterminate case.

Where a sufficient number of clumps yield resolved, non-limit sizes, clump size will be plotted against linewidth to construct a size–linewidth relation for the sample. This relation will be compared qualitatively to established size–linewidth relations from Galactic and extragalactic molecular cloud studies, noting where the NGC 3256 outflow clumps fall relative to these reference trends.

### Step 8: Local ratio comparison at the clump scale

For each clump identified in Step 5, the local HCN/HCO+ ratio will be computed using the same masked, matched-beam moment-0 maps constructed for that MOUS following the Step 2 procedure, but evaluated only over the clump aperture rather than over the full outflow mask. Each clump's local ratio will be compared to the global outflow-versus-disk ratio contrast established in Step 4, using the 0.8″ result as the nearest-resolution point of comparison, since the 0.237″ dataset alone lacks the signal-to-noise and field coverage needed for a full disk map. The comparison will note whether clumps showing local line-ratio enhancement also correspond to marginally resolved, compact clumps — the observational signature expected under the compression-driven star formation hypothesis motivating this study.

### Step 9: Synthesis of deliverables

The completed analysis will assemble the following set of results: (1) HCN(3–2)/HCO+(3–2) ratio maps together with their companion uncertainty maps at 4.352″ and 0.8″ resolution; (2) position–velocity diagrams along the identified kinematic axis for both HCN and HCO+; (3) the disk-versus-outflow ratio contrast and its statistical significance at both coarser resolutions; (4) a table of clump positions, fitted or upper-limit sizes, linewidths, and virial masses (or mass limits) derived from the 0.237″ data; (5) a size–linewidth plot, where a sufficient number of resolved clumps permit one; and (6) a table comparing local clump-scale line ratios to the global outflow-versus-disk ratio contrast. All results will be framed explicitly as a first-order chemical and kinematic diagnostic of the outflow, distinguished from — and intended to complement rather than reproduce — a full multi-line HCN/HCO+ LVG density and kinetic-temperature analysis of the same system.

## 4. Conclusions

This study is designed to test whether the HCN(3–2)/HCO+(3–2) line ratio in the molecular outflow of NGC 3256 is distinguishable from that of the disk, and whether any such distinction coincides spatially with compact, marginally resolved clumps consistent with a compression-driven star formation scenario. Using archival Band 6 ALMA data from a single project imaged identically for both lines at three matched resolutions, the approach compares outflow- and disk-associated gas at two coarser scales via masked, matched-beam ratio maps and kinematic/spatial masking, and then examines individual clumps at the highest resolution through size, linewidth, virial mass, and local ratio measurements. As stated at the outset, no analysis has yet been performed on real data, and the Results section above is a placeholder; the conclusions that follow are therefore necessarily conditional and describe what different possible outcomes would imply, not what has been found.

If the HCN/HCO+ ratio is found to be enhanced in outflow-flagged gas relative to disk gas, and this enhancement is consistent in sign and rough magnitude across the 4.352″ and 0.8″ datasets, this would provide two independent, internally cross-checked lines of evidence that the outflowing gas occupies a chemically or excitation-wise distinct regime from the disk. If, in addition, the highest-resolution clumps identified within the outflow show locally elevated ratios that track their compactness, and if a size–linewidth relation for these clumps (where enough resolved sizes are obtained) falls above established Galactic and extragalactic trends in a manner suggestive of elevated surface density, the combined result would be consistent with — though not proof of — a compression-driven chemical signature operating at clump scales within the outflow, complementary to more detailed excitation modeling of this system.

Conversely, if no statistically distinguishable ratio contrast is found between outflow and disk gas at either resolution, or if a contrast is found at one resolution but not reproduced at the other, this would argue against a robust, scale-independent chemical signature of compression and would instead suggest that any apparent enhancement is either an artifact of a particular angular scale, a masking choice, or noise, or that the outflowing gas is not chemically distinct from the disk at the sensitivity of this dataset. Similarly, if clumps are identified at high resolution but their local ratios do not track the global outflow-versus-disk contrast, or if too few clumps yield resolved (non-limit) sizes to construct a meaningful size–linewidth relation, this would limit the extent to which any global signal could be tied specifically to compact sub-structure, and would leave open whether compression, if present, operates at scales not resolved by the 0.237″ beam. It is also possible that velocity-offset emission attributed to the outflow is instead more plausibly associated with tidal debris, a common feature of a late-stage merger such as NGC 3256; if the spatial and kinematic properties of the flagged emission are more consistent with literature descriptions of tidal features than of the outflow, this would undercut the interpretation of any measured ratio contrast as outflow-related at all, regardless of its statistical significance.

Because the HCN/HCO+ ratio is being used here explicitly as a first-order chemical and kinematic proxy rather than as a substitute for a full non-LTE density and temperature analysis, none of the outcomes described above, whether or not a ratio enhancement is detected, whether or not it coincides with clump-scale structure, can by itself establish the underlying physical mechanism (compression versus shocks, irradiation, or other excitation effects) with certainty. Any result obtained will need to be framed as a constraint that is consistent or inconsistent with the compression-driven star formation hypothesis, to be weighed alongside, and ideally followed up by, more detailed multi-line excitation modeling of the same outflow.
