**Title:** Mapping the HCN/HCO+(J=3–2) Line Ratio and Clump Properties in the NGC 3256 Outflow: A Chemical and Kinematic Diagnostic of Compression-Driven Star Formation

**Data used:** Only the Band 6 member OUS of project 2017.1.00379.S (uid://A001/X1273/Xb4b, Xb4d, Xb4f), which cover HCN(3–2) and HCO+(3–2) simultaneously at three angular resolutions (4.352″, 0.8″, 0.237″) from pipeline-calibrated cubes already in the archive. No other bands, lines, or external catalogs are required.

**Scope:**

1. **Global ratio mapping (4.352″ and 0.8″ cubes, each analyzed at its own native resolution):** Within each cube, HCN(3–2) and HCO+(3–2) share a common beam (same MOUS, same observing setup), so the ratio map is internally consistent without any cross-resolution convolution step. Build moment-0 maps of both lines using a common mask (e.g., a fixed S/N threshold applied to one line's integrated-intensity map, applied identically to both) and a shared noise cutoff, then take the ratio only where both lines are detected above that cutoff — pixels failing this test are left blank rather than extrapolated. Produce a position–velocity diagram along the merger's large-scale kinematic axis (read directly off the observed velocity field, not fit). Define "outflow gas" operationally as emission in velocity channels significantly offset from the ~2800 km/s systemic velocity and spatially offset from the main emission ridge — a simple, literature-standard criterion, not a from-scratch kinematic decomposition. Compute the HCN/HCO+ ratio separately for outflow-defined gas and for systemic-velocity (disk) gas at each resolution independently, testing whether the outflow shows an enhanced ratio relative to the disk. State explicitly that this ratio is a chemistry/excitation-conditions proxy, not a density or kinetic-temperature measurement — it can show tension with or support for the PI's compression hypothesis, but it does not substitute for the full three-rung HCN/HCO+ LVG ladder the PI's proposal uses to derive density and star-formation efficiency. The thesis also notes, as a stated limitation, that some velocity-offset emission in a late-stage merger could in principle be tidal debris rather than outflow, discussed against the outflow geometry reported in the PI's proposal but not independently re-derived.

2. **Clump-scale follow-up (0.237″ cube):** In the same two lines at high resolution, identify 3–5 discrete bright clumps within the outflow-velocity channels, and measure their deconvolved angular sizes (2D Gaussian fits) and velocity linewidths (1D line-profile fits) directly from the cube. At NGC 3256's distance, 0.237″ corresponds to tens of pc, comparable to plausible individual clump sizes, so most detections will be only marginally resolved; the thesis explicitly reports fitted sizes and any derived size–linewidth relation or virial mass as order-of-magnitude estimates only, flagging that deconvolution errors are strongly correlated with the marginal beam-to-source size ratio. Where a clump's fitted FWHM does not exceed the beam within its uncertainty, only an upper limit on size (and corresponding lower limit on any density inference) is reported — this is treated as a valid, bounded finding, not a problem requiring further phases. Local HCN/HCO+ ratios at clump positions are compared to the global outflow value from step 1.

**Deliverable:** A ratio map and quantitative disk-vs-outflow HCN/HCO+ contrast in NGC 3256 at two independent resolutions, plus resolved or upper-limit clump sizes, linewidths, and order-of-magnitude virial mass estimates with a local ratio comparison — framed as a first-order chemical/kinematic diagnostic that tests for compression-consistent signatures in outflowing dense clumps relative to disk gas, complementary to (not a replacement for) the PI's full LVG density analysis. Achieved using one internally consistent spectral setup and standard CASA imaging/fitting techniques (moment maps, PV diagrams, Gaussian fitting, virial mass estimation).
