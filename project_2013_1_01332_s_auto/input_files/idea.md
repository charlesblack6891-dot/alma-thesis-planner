**Title:** Mapping Interaction-Driven Dense-Gas Chemistry Across the NGC 4567/4568 Tidal Overlap Zone

**Data used:** The pipeline-calibrated ALMA Band 3 measurement set / QA2 image cubes from project 2013.1.01332.S (member OUS `uid://A001/X144/X81`), covering HCN J=1–0, HCO+ J=1–0, HNC J=1–0, and CCH N=1–0 toward VV_219 (NGC 4567/4568) at ~1.14″ (~90 pc) resolution. No new observations, external instrumentation, or ancillary datasets are required; all four lines are already present in the delivered spectral windows.

**Idea:** NGC 4567/4568 is a Virgo-Cluster galaxy pair caught in the act of merging, with a tidal "overlap" region where the two disks physically collide, distinct from each galaxy's separate nuclear and disk gas. The thesis builds moment-0 (integrated intensity) maps for each of the four dense-gas/PDR tracers directly from the archival cubes (re-imaging in CASA only if the delivered QA2 products need cleaning refinement), then constructs spatially resolved line-ratio maps — HCN/HCO+, HCN/HNC, and HCN/CCH — across the system.

Before any imaging or ratio work, the student runs a data-quality check specific to this being a 12-m-array-only observation (no ACA/total-power data in this member OUS): pull the maximum recoverable scale (MRS) implied by the delivered array configuration/uv-coverage (from the QA2 report or the measurement set's uv-distance distribution) and compare it to the angular extent of the candidate nuclei, disk, and bridge apertures. This determines whether disk-scale (more extended) emission is at risk of resolved-out flux relative to compact bridge/nuclear emission — a risk that would differentially suppress diffuse tracers like CCH relative to compact tracers and could manufacture or mask the very nuclei/disk-vs-bridge contrast the thesis tests. This check is reported explicitly, and the result is carried into the final interpretation as a named caveat regardless of outcome (not just when it is a problem).

Region boundaries are defined empirically by the student directly from the ALMA continuum and HCN moment-0 maps: continuum/line peaks mark the two nuclei, the morphologically distinct connecting structure between the disks marks the bridge, and the remaining extended emission is the disk gas, using a fixed intensity contour or peak-finding criterion documented as a reproducible methods step. An SNR triage (≥5σ per beam within each candidate aperture, threshold fixed in advance) determines which tracers are usable in each region; if CCH and/or HNC fail in the bridge, the project falls back to a coarser two-region comparison (combined nuclei/disk vs. bridge) rather than dropping weak-line results post hoc.

Statistical comparison across regions uses independent-beam sampling (one value per beam area, not per-pixel) as the unit for a non-parametric comparison (Mann-Whitney/KS), with any region too small for several independent beams merged or excluded. Given that this single dataset likely yields only 2–3 usable regions and a modest beam count per region, the thesis explicitly treats this test as a supplementary, low-power confirmatory check rather than the primary basis for a claim — the main interpretive weight rests on the moment-0/line-ratio maps themselves and descriptive effect sizes, so the write-up is not over-invested in formal significance testing that a low-N dataset cannot really support.

The scientific question this addresses is the one the proposal itself was built to answer at low-z: whether tidally driven compression/shocks in the overlap region measurably alter dense-gas chemistry and excitation relative to undisturbed disk gas, as a local analog for the dense-gas physics invoked in high-z mergers. The limitations section covers three caveats: (1) single-transition (J=1–0 only) line ratios cannot cleanly separate excitation, optical depth, and abundance effects, so ratio shifts are interpreted as a chemistry/excitation proxy, not unambiguous evidence of one mechanism; (2) beam-sized sampling reduces gross pseudoreplication but does not guarantee i.i.d. draws, since line ratios may vary along smooth spatial gradients rather than showing a clean regional contrast; and (3) as a 12-m-only observation, missing short-spacing flux can differentially suppress extended/diffuse emission (and diffuse tracers like CCH) relative to compact emission, which must be weighed against the MRS check before any nuclei/disk-vs-bridge contrast is attributed to chemistry or excitation rather than to a resolution-dependent flux-recovery artifact.

The entire analysis — MRS/missing-flux check, calibrated-data retrieval, empirical region definition, pre-registered SNR triage, moment-map generation, line-ratio computation, and appropriately scoped region-based comparison — is achievable by one student in a semester-to-year thesis using only CASA and the archival products, with no dependence on the VIRUS-P IFU data, HI/CO maps, or GALEX/Spitzer/Herschel/HST imagery mentioned as ancillary context in the proposal (those remain optional qualitative overlays only, not quantitative inputs).
