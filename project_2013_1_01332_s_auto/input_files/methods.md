**1. Data acquisition and inspection**

The student begins by downloading the full data package for project 2013.1.01332.S, member OUS `uid://A001/X144/X81`, from the ALMA Science Archive: the pipeline-calibrated measurement set (or the raw ASDM plus calibration scripts, if re-calibration proves necessary) and the QA2 image cube products. The student reads the QA2 report delivered with the data to record the achieved synthesized beam, sensitivity, spectral windows, and any pipeline flags or quality caveats. The four spectral windows are identified against the line list in the QA2 header and cross-checked against Splatalogue at the system redshift (z ≈ 0.0075) to confirm which windows contain HCN J=1–0, HCO+ J=1–0, HNC J=1–0, and CCH N=1–0, and to note that the two windows near 99–102 GHz are continuum-dominated with only a marginal HC3N J=11–10 feature possible.

**2. Maximum recoverable scale / missing-flux assessment**

Before any science imaging, the student performs a dedicated data-quality check specific to the fact that this is a 12-m-array-only observation with no ACA or total-power data. Using CASA (`plotms` or direct inspection of the measurement set), the student extracts the uv-distance distribution and determines the shortest sampled baseline. From this, the student computes the maximum recoverable scale (MRS ≈ 0.6 λ / b_min, following the standard ALMA technical handbook formula) and compares it to the angular sizes of the candidate structures to be studied (nuclei, bridge, disk), estimated from the continuum and HCN moment-0 maps produced in Step 3. This comparison is written up explicitly as a standalone methods result, stating whether disk-scale emission is at risk of resolved-out flux relative to compact nuclear/bridge emission, and is carried forward as a named caveat in the interpretation regardless of the outcome.

**3. Imaging**

The student uses CASA (`tclean`) to produce spectral-line cubes for each of the four tracers. The delivered QA2 cubes are used as the starting point; the student inspects them for residual artifacts (poor deconvolution, sidelobes, missed cleaning) and re-images from the calibrated measurement set only where necessary, using briggs weighting (robust ~0.5, matching the delivered angular resolution of ~1.14″) and a common restoring beam across all four lines so that line-ratio maps are computed on a matched-resolution grid. Continuum is imaged separately from line-free channels in the two upper spectral windows for use in identifying nuclear positions. For each line cube, the student subtracts continuum (`uvcontsub` or `imcontsub`) before generating moment maps.

**4. Moment map generation**

For each of the four tracers, the student uses CASA's `immoments` task to generate moment-0 (integrated intensity) maps, masking on a fixed signal-to-noise threshold (e.g., 3–5σ per channel, applied consistently across all four cubes) to avoid including noise channels. Moment-0 maps are converted to consistent flux/brightness units (Jy/beam km/s) so that line ratios can be computed directly as ratios of integrated intensity.

**5. Empirical region definition**

Using the continuum map and the HCN moment-0 map (the strongest, most extended tracer), the student defines three regions with a documented, reproducible criterion: (a) the two nuclei, marked by continuum and/or HCN peak positions above a fixed threshold; (b) the bridge, the morphologically distinct connecting structure of HCN emission between the two disks, defined by a fixed intensity contour level; and (c) the disk, the remaining extended HCN emission outside the nuclei and bridge apertures. The exact contour levels and peak-finding parameters used are recorded in the methods so the region definitions are reproducible from the moment-0 maps alone.

**6. Signal-to-noise triage**

For each of the three regions and each of the four tracers, the student measures the per-beam SNR of the moment-0 map (using the RMS in line-free cube channels, propagated through the moment-0 integration). A pre-registered threshold (≥5σ per beam) determines whether a given tracer is usable in a given region. If CCH and/or HNC fail this threshold in the bridge (the faintest region), the student falls back to a coarser two-region scheme (combined nuclei+disk vs. bridge) for those tracers, rather than discarding them from the analysis entirely.

**7. Line-ratio map construction**

For every pair of tracers passing the SNR triage in a given region, the student constructs spatially resolved ratio maps (HCN/HCO+, HCN/HNC, HCN/CCH) by dividing the co-registered, common-resolution moment-0 maps pixel-by-pixel, propagating errors from the per-pixel RMS of each input map. Ratio maps are masked wherever either input tracer falls below the SNR threshold. The student tabulates the median and interquartile range of each ratio within each defined region (nuclei, bridge, disk) as the primary descriptive result.

**8. Region-based statistical comparison**

To test whether line ratios differ significantly between regions, the student samples one value per independent synthesized beam area within each region (rather than per pixel) to avoid pseudoreplication from correlated adjacent pixels. Any region containing too few independent beams for a meaningful sample is merged with an adjacent region or excluded from the quantitative test, following the two-region fallback in Step 6 if needed. The student applies a non-parametric test (Mann-Whitney U or Kolmogorov-Smirnov) to compare the beam-sampled ratio distributions between regions (e.g., bridge vs. disk, nuclei vs. disk), explicitly treating this as a supplementary, low-power confirmatory check rather than the primary evidence, given the small number of usable regions and modest beam counts. The main interpretive weight is placed on the moment-0 and line-ratio maps themselves and on descriptive effect sizes (median ratio differences and their spread), not on formal p-values.

**9. Interpretation**

The student interprets any observed spatial variation in the HCN/HCO+, HCN/HNC, and HCN/CCH ratios across the nuclei, bridge, and disk regions in terms of the physical picture motivating the proposal: tidally driven compression and shocks in the overlap region altering the dense-gas fraction, excitation, and chemistry relative to undisturbed disk gas. This interpretation is qualified against three considerations documented alongside the results: that single-transition (J=1–0 only) ratios cannot cleanly separate excitation, optical depth, and abundance effects, so ratio shifts are treated as a joint chemistry/excitation proxy; that beam-sampling reduces but does not eliminate spatial autocorrelation, since ratios may vary smoothly rather than showing sharp regional contrast; and that the MRS/missing-flux assessment from Step 2 is weighed explicitly against any nuclei/disk-vs-bridge contrast before attributing it to chemistry or excitation rather than to differential resolved-out flux, particularly for the more extended tracer CCH. All CASA scripts, region definitions, thresholds, and imaging parameters are retained and documented so that the full pipeline — from calibrated visibilities to final ratio maps and regional comparison — is reproducible from the archival data alone.
